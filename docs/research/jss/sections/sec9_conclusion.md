# 9. Conclusion

Typed, QoS-aware architecture models support accurate pre-deployment ranking of components by cascading-failure impact, without runtime telemetry. On twelve held-out synthetic architectures, SaG’s QoS-aware projection raises closed-form ranking from $\rho = 0.349$ to $0.553$ on every fold. A control matched in capacity and edge-channel width shows that the QoS edge encoding, not relation-specific weights, is what learned engines need. On their own, learned engines are statistically on par with the closed-form engine. Hybrid engines, in which a learned engine corrects the closed-form score, significantly outperform it ($\rho = 0.657$ and $0.683$, on 11 of 12 folds under registered decision rules). Pure learned engines transfer best to independently authored system models ($\rho = 0.760$–$0.805$ against $0.511$–$0.526$).

For practice, SaG offers a training-free closed-form engine for lightweight CI gates, a hybrid engine for familiar architectures, a QoS-aware learned engine for substantially different systems, and an ISO/IEC 25010 attribution layer that names the remediation each flagged component calls for. The next steps are independent re-modeling of the system models, retargeting on the behavioral queue-flow oracle, extraction of models from deployment manifests, and validation against production incidents.
