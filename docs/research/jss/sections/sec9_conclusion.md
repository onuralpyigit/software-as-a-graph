# 9. Conclusion

This study showed that typed, QoS-aware architecture models support accurate pre-deployment ranking of components by cascading-failure impact, without runtime telemetry. Software-as-a-Graph turns architecture descriptions into typed multigraphs with QoS-weighted dependencies, and three engines built on this representation were evaluated under leave-one-scenario-out cross-validation over twelve synthetic architectures and zero-shot on five independently authored models of open-source systems.

The representation carries much of the signal: QoS-aware projection raises closed-form ranking from $\rho = 0.349$ to $0.553$ on every held-out architecture. Heterogeneous learning adds relational reasoning that untyped models lack ($+0.234$ over an untyped GNN on all twelve folds), and it transfers well beyond its training distribution ($\rho = 0.760$ against $0.51$–$0.53$ on the system models, with nearly double the top-$K$ critical-set overlap). Learned and closed-form reasoning are complementary. SaG-Hybrid, in which the transformer learns a correction to the closed-form score, is the best engine on unseen synthetic architectures ($\rho = 0.657$) and significantly outperforms closed-form ranking on 11 of 12 folds ($+0.103$, Holm $p = 0.0068$), as registered before the experiment. An ISO/IEC 25010 attribution layer complements the rankings by naming the remediation each flagged component calls for.

For practice, SaG offers a training-free closed-form engine for lightweight CI gates, the hybrid engine for the most accurate ranking, and the pure learned engine for transfer to substantially different systems. The next steps are to retarget the engines on the behavioral queue-flow oracle, to extract system models directly from deployment manifests, and to validate the rankings against production incident data.
