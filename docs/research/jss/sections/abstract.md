# Abstract

Publish–subscribe middleware is fundamental to modern distributed systems, but its decoupled topic abstractions frequently obscure cascading failure paths. Software-as-a-Graph (SaG) models event-driven architectures as typed multigraphs and derives explicit dependency graphs from manifests. This study evaluates learned cascade-impact rankers—graph neural networks, hybrids, and surrogates—on twelve synthetic architectures under leave-one-scenario-out cross-validation, against three failure simulators, and zero-shot on five open-source system models.

Deriving dependency graphs enables graph learning: graph attention networks gain $+0.08$ to $+0.11$ over the raw multigraph, reaching Spearman $\rho = 0.748$ against reachability simulation. This is statistically indistinguishable from direct dependent counts ($0.764$, restating the simulator’s first propagation wave) and relies on degree features. Learning pays off where simulation is costly: on a dynamic queue-flow simulator requiring $12.7$ CPU-hours to label the corpus, a gradient-boosted surrogate ranks held-out architectures above unweighted closed-form approximations ($0.799$ vs. $0.706$; nominal $p = 0.019$), replacing hours of simulation with inference in seconds. While an analytical rate-weighted expansion reaches $\rho = 0.830$ without training, the surrogate captures non-linear queue saturation. Under multi-criteria fragmentation, hybrids retain accuracy via closed-form priors ($\rho = 0.585$ vs. $\le 0.334$). Pure learned models transfer best to unfamiliar systems ($\rho = 0.805$ vs. $0.662$–$0.695$), primarily separating inert from active components; ranking within active components remains challenging ($\rho_{>0} \le 0.342$). The results support an energy-aware two-tier approach: dependency counting for commit-stage triage, and learned surrogates for staging verification ($\sim 355.6$ Wh theoretical TDP proxy).

**Keywords:** Dependency graphs; cascading failures; publish–subscribe; graph neural networks; graph learning; software architecture; dependability; empirical study
