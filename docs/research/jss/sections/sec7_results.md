## 6.1 RQ1: Ranking Accuracy Against Structural Baselines

**Summary.** *Against $I^*$, the strongest rankings are references that restate its first propagation wave (first-order expansion $0.808$, direct dependents $0.764$). The registered primary contrast, `HGT-QoS` against `Topo-QoS` ($0.553$), is null. The two hybrids beat `Topo-QoS` on 11 of 12 folds (Holm $p = 0.0068$ and $0.0029$), but not their own base learners, and only Hybrid-GAT beats unweighted betweenness after correction. The best GNN is a GAT on the dependency graph ($0.748$); it is statistically indistinguishable from the direct-dependent count, but not equivalent to it within $\pm 0.05$, and it depends on its degree features (§6.2). On full-population $I_{\text{dyn}}$ no GNN reaches the references, while a gradient-boosted surrogate trained on $I_{\text{dyn}}$ exceeds them; on $I_{\text{comp}}$ closed-form centrality and raw degree, which are proximate to its terms, rank highest. Recovering $80\%$ of the true top-$20\%$ set requires flagging the top $40\%$ by `GAT-P-QoS`.*

Learned engines are trained on eleven scenarios and scored on the twelfth; training-free rankers are simply scored on each of the twelve (26 to 300 Applications, $K$ between 5 and 60). **The reference level.** By Remark 1, `InDeg` is publish–subscribe afferent coupling [23, 24, 60] and counts exactly the Applications that $I^*$’s first propagation wave reaches; `Reach` counts the transitive dependents a reachability cascade can visit. Their values on $I^*$ ($0.764$, $\rho_{>0} = 0.516$; $0.732$) therefore measure how much of the oracle a restatement of its rule recovers, not predictive skill, and the same holds for their raw-graph equivalents: counting the topics a component publishes (Pubs-raw) reaches $0.731$, and the raw publisher $\to$ topic $\to$ subscriber closure (Reach-R1) $0.674$. Among the references, the derivation contributes in one measured place: without Rule 5, `Reach` falls by $0.058$ (9/12 folds, Holm $p = 0.0068$; Amendment 10). Untyped raw-graph metrics recover none of the reference: total degree reaches $0.199$ and reverse PageRank $0.089$, and PageRank is constant for every Application because Applications have no incoming raw edges.

**Learners on the dependency graph approach the reference.** The same graph neural networks, trained on the dependency graph instead of the raw multigraph, reach $\rho = 0.748$ (`GAT-P-QoS`), the best GNN in Table 6, and $0.016$ below the direct-dependent count on the per-seed statistic; its five-seed ensemble is $0.007$ above it ($0.772$, Table 7). Neither difference is significant, and neither is equivalence: the $90\%$ interval of the per-fold difference reaches $\pm 0.076$, so a two one-sided test at the registered margin of $\pm 0.05$ fails ($p = 0.17$ per seed, $0.13$ for the ensemble; Amendment 14, F3). The learner and the count are therefore indistinguishable at this sample size, not ranked. The approach to the reference rests on the degree features every learner receives (§3.5): removing `in_degree` and $w_{\text{in}}$ lowers `GAT-P-QoS` to $0.612$ (§6.2). A variant that uses `InDeg` as its prior is reported only in Supplementary §S34, because its prior is a reference. `HGT-P-QoS` reaches only $0.514$ and is highly seed-dependent (mean within-fold seed SD $0.254$; §7.3).

**Hybrids outperform the registered comparator, and little else.** Hybrid-HGT reaches $\rho = 0.657$ ($+0.103$, Holm $p = 0.0068$) and Hybrid-GAT $0.683$ ($+0.130$, Holm $p = 0.0029$) against `Topo-QoS`, each on 11 of 12 folds; both stay significant under the omnibus Holm correction ($p_{\text{omni}} = 0.041$ and $0.019$), under the Nadeau–Bengio corrected $t$ ($p = 0.019$ and $0.014$) and under $|V_{\text{app}}|$-weighted averaging (exact sign-flip $p = 0.038$ and $0.005$; Supplementary §S36). They are the study’s only significant registered improvements, and they are weaker than they look, because a hybrid receives $p(v)$ as an input and adds a learned correction to its logit, so it nests its comparator. Against their own base learners, which Amendments 5 and 6 registered alongside, neither is significant (Hybrid-HGT vs. `HGT-QoS` $+0.035$, $p = 0.73$; Hybrid-GAT vs. `GAT-QoS` $+0.048$, $p = 0.30$). Against the two stronger training-free controls of Amendment 7 (Amendment 14, F7), Hybrid-GAT keeps a margin (unweighted betweenness on the same graph $+0.092$, constant topic weights $+0.088$, both Holm $p = 0.049$), Hybrid-HGT does not ($+0.065$ and $+0.061$, Holm $p = 0.085$). The prior also costs transfer: zero-shot, both hybrids fall below their base learners (§6.3). On their own, the raw-multigraph learned engines do not differ significantly from `Topo-QoS` (`HGT-QoS` $+0.069$, $p = 0.266$, the null primary contrast; `GAT-QoS` $+0.082$, $p = 0.233$).

**Where the closed-form gain comes from.** `Topo` reads betweenness from the application-layer graph, whereas `Topo-QoS` computes QoS-weighted betweenness on the Application–Library dependency graph. Three controls registered in Amendment 7 separate substrate from weighting: unweighted betweenness on the same graph scores $0.591$; constant topic weights score $0.595$; permuting QoS profiles scores $0.559$ ($-0.006$, $p = 0.73$). On $I^*$, the gain comes from the Application–Library graph structure, not from QoS contract content.

**The first-order expansion of the oracle.** The strongest reference is a post hoc closed-form first-order expansion of $I^*$: $$\tag{6}
\hat{I}^*_1(v) = \sum_{t \in \text{pub}(v)} \frac{|\text{sub}(t)|}{|\text{pub}(t)|},$$ where $|\text{pub}(t)| \ge 1$ holds for all published topics $t \in \text{pub}(v)$ by construction ($v$ publishes to $t$), preventing any division by zero. It reaches mean $\rho = 0.808$ $[0.755, 0.853]$ ($\rho_{>0} = 0.631$): direct subscriber loss is most of what $I^*$ measures, and `InDeg` is a coarser version of the same quantity. As a reference it bounds the mean rather than every fold: on four folds (AV, Financial Trading, Healthcare, Industrial SCADA) `InDeg` slightly exceeds it (by $0.005$–$0.038$).

**Beyond the reachability oracle.** Table 7 scores the rankers against the two other oracles. On full-population $I_{\text{dyn}}$, which agrees with $I^*$ at $\rho = 0.711$ $[0.625, 0.794]$, the references stay ahead of every GNN (first-order expansion $0.706$, `InDeg` $0.664$, against $0.496$–$0.615$), and `Topo-QoS` reaches $0.471$. Because $I_{\text{dyn}}$ shares the topology and the first-order effect with $I^*$, the partial correlation shows how much of each ranking survives outside the oracle it was fitted to or restates. After the rank of $I^*$ is removed, `InDeg` keeps $0.272$ $[0.188, 0.366]$, `Reach` $0.117$, `Topo-QoS` $0.134$ and the GNNs $0.107$–$0.173$, every interval excluding zero. After the first-order expansion is removed instead, the order reverses: `InDeg` keeps $0.079$, and the raw-multigraph GNNs keep most ($0.212$–$0.217$), so the learners carry queue-flow signal beyond the direct count that the count does not. Learning from the right labels matters more than the graph model. A gradient-boosted model over the counts, the first-order expansion and QoS features, trained on $I_{\text{dyn}}$ labels of the training scenarios, ranks held-out architectures at $0.799$, above the first-order expansion ($+0.094$, Holm $p = 0.019$) and `InDeg` ($+0.135$, Holm $p = 0.019$; Amendment 11, Family B); its QoS features carry that gain ($+0.095$ over the same model without them, Holm $p = 0.014$), whereas on $I^*$ they add nothing ($-0.003$, $p = 0.62$; Family C). The GNN given the same labels does not: `GAT-P-QoS`$\to$dyn reaches $0.598$, below the first-order expansion ($-0.108$, Holm $p = 0.014$) and the gradient-boosted surrogate ($-0.201$, Holm $p = 0.0015$), and no better than the same GNN trained on $I^*$ ($-0.008$; Amendment 14, F5). These surrogates are trained on the oracle they are scored on; their value is cost, since labeling $I_{\text{dyn}}$ took $12.7$ CPU-hours for the corpus (§6.4). Trained on $I^*$ instead, the same gradient-boosted model is less robust across the three oracles than the first-order expansion alone (worst-case $\rho$ $-0.121$, Holm $p = 0.001$; Family A).

**The multi-criteria oracle favors different rankers.** On $I_{\text{comp}}$, which adds fragmentation, throughput and flow disruption (§4.3), QoS-weighted centrality ranks above the direct-dependent reference ($\rho = 0.702$ $[0.649, 0.753]$ against $0.650$; the reference ahead on only 3 of 12 folds, $p = 0.110$), and raw total degree, the weakest ranker on $I^*$, reaches $0.719$ (the reference ahead on 4 of 12 folds, Holm $p = 0.220$), while `Reach` falls to $0.302$. Both leaders are proximate to $I_{\text{comp}}$’s fragmentation and flow terms (§4.4), so their lead is read as the same kind of overlap as the references’ lead on $I^*$, not as predictive skill. The learned engines without a centrality prior collapse on this oracle ($0.144$–$0.334$; the sum-aggregation GNNs of §6.2 reach $0.41$–$0.44$); the two hybrids, which carry `Topo-QoS` as a prior, keep $0.58$. No ranker is best on all three oracles.

**Identifying the critical set.** Rank correlation over all Applications is not the operational question in a CI gate, which is whether the true top of the ranking gets flagged. Figure 5 gives, for each ranker, the share of the true top-$20\%$ set on $I^*$ that falls within its top $k\%$, with ties resolved in expectation (Amendment 12). At $k = 20\%$, `GAT-P-QoS` recovers $0.48$ of the critical set and `Topo-QoS` $0.38$, consistent with their Overlap@$K$ ($0.454$ and $0.388$): about half of what a top-$20\%$ gate flags is not in the true top $20\%$, and about half of the true top $20\%$ is missed. Recovering $80\%$ of the critical set requires flagging the top $40\%$ by `GAT-P-QoS` ($0.81$), and `Topo-QoS` does not reach $80\%$ within $50\%$. The references do little better: at $k = 20\%$, `InDeg` recovers $0.49$ (tie-breaking bounds $0.48$–$0.50$), and $80\%$ needs the top $45\%$ by `InDeg` ($0.83$) or by the first-order expansion ($0.89$); only the first-order expansion reaches $90\%$ within the top $50\%$, and `Reach` does not reach $80\%$ within $50\%$. On the earlier $n = 30$ $I_{\text{dyn}}$ sample (the right panel of Figure 5; not recomputed on the full population), `GAT-P-QoS` reaches $0.65$ at $40\%$ and $0.80$ at $50\%$, against $0.83$ at $40\%$ for the `InDeg` reference.

## 6.2 RQ2: What Learned Engines Need

**Summary.** *Learned engines gain $+0.08$ to $+0.11$ from reading the dependency graph, where messages reach the scored Applications from their dependents (11–12 of 12 folds). What brings the dependency-graph GAT close to the reference is its degree features: without them it loses $0.14$ (Holm $p = 0.005$). A sum-aggregation GNN, which can count dependents, loses only $0.01$ without them, although the aggregator contrast itself is not significant after correction. With capacity matched, relation typing adds nothing; with the weighted in-degree held in both arms, the “QoS” inputs add nothing measurable either ($+0.030$, Holm $p = 0.33$).*

**The “QoS” factor was mostly the weighted in-degree.** In Table 8 its main effect ($+0.073$) is not significant after Holm correction ($p_{\text{Holm}} = 0.127$), and it could not be attributed to QoS content in any case: $w_{\text{in}}$, the sum of incoming dependency weights, is a QoS-weighted version of `InDeg`, and the QoS-off arms zeroed it. Amendment 14 reran the matched $2\times2$ with $w_{\text{in}}$ kept in the QoS-off cells (Table 9, F4): the main effect falls to $+0.030$ $[-0.023, +0.085]$ (Holm $p = 0.33$), typing is $-0.036$ and the interaction $+0.046$, none significant. More than half of the earlier QoS effect was therefore the weighted in-degree. The closed-form controls point the same way: unweighted betweenness ($0.591$) and constant topic weights ($0.595$) match or exceed QoS-weighted betweenness ($0.553$), and permuting QoS profiles across topics changes nothing ($\Delta = -0.006$ $[-0.025, +0.015]$, $p = 0.733$). On $I^*$ we find no evidence that QoS contract content carries ranking signal; on $I_{\text{dyn}}$, whose mechanics read the contracts, it does (§6.1, Amendment 11 Family C).

**What the dependency-graph learner needs.** Every learner receives in-degree and $w_{\text{in}}$ as node features, and on the dependency graph it also aggregates exactly the dependent neighborhood, so its closeness to `InDeg` could come from either. Amendment 14 separates the two (Table 9). Removing the two degree columns costs `GAT-P-QoS` $0.136$, and removing the three centralities most correlated with in-degree as well costs $0.161$ (F1, rule F1a): the approach to the reference depends on the degree features. That is what message-passing theory predicts for this architecture: softmax attention normalizes over neighbors and computes a weighted mean, which cannot count them, whereas a sum aggregator can [76, 77]. A GINE network of the same parameter budget bears this out descriptively, keeping $0.721$ and $0.711$ without the same columns, $0.01$–$0.02$ below its own full-feature score, but the aggregator contrasts miss significance after Holm correction ($+0.108$ and $+0.124$, $p_{\text{Holm}} = 0.157$; rule F2b), so the study does not claim an aggregator effect. No dependency-graph learner, with or without degree features, is equivalent to `InDeg` within $\pm 0.05$ (equivalence bounds $0.063$–$0.276$; F3). Zero-shot, the degree columns matter little ($0.810$–$0.821$ without them), because on the system models most of the ranking is separating inert from active components (§6.3).

**Relation typing under matched capacity.** `HGT-QoS` remains within $0.013$ of `GAT-QoS` (Table 8). On the raw multigraph this says little about typing, because no message reaches an Application there: a gradient-boosted model over the same per-node features without any graph (`GBM-Feat`, Amendment 8) reaches $0.642$, level with `GAT-QoS`, and removing HGT’s reverse pass, the only route by which messages reach Applications, changes nothing (`HGT-QoS-U`, $-0.010$, $p = 0.91$; Supplementary §S25). On the dependency graph, where messages do arrive, `HGT-P-QoS` reaches $0.514$ with a mean within-fold seed SD of $0.254$, against $0.748$ and $0.030$ for `GAT-P-QoS`. This is one fixed configuration (width 100), and averaging its five seeds’ predictions raises it to $0.618$; we therefore read it as seed instability of that configuration, not as evidence against relational typing in general. **The registered selection rule.** The plan chose every hyperparameter by nested selection, which the published results did not apply (§5.3). Run afterwards on the stage-1 grid (Amendment 14, arm N), it moves `HGT-QoS` from $0.622$ to $0.677$ ($+0.055$) and `GAT-P-QoS` from $0.748$ to $0.693$ ($-0.054$), neither significant (Holm $p = 0.259$); nested `HGT-QoS` against `Topo-QoS` is $+0.123$ on 9 of 12 folds, still not significant (Holm $p = 0.192$; rule F6b). Applying the registered rule therefore does not change the primary result. Table 6 retains the fixed, published configuration as the primary benchmark, while Arm N serves strictly as an exploratory sensitivity analysis confirming that hyperparameter tuning on the stage-1 grid does not overturn the null primary contrast. The search harness also early-stops on a held-out training scenario rather than on the published node-level split, and on the three folds where it chose the published hyperparameters it does not reproduce the published scores (up to $0.10$ apart), so these differences cannot be attributed to hyperparameter selection alone (Supplementary §S36).

## 6.3 RQ3: Zero-Shot Transfer to Models of Open-Source Systems

**Summary.** *On five hand-authored models of open-source systems, the best learned engines rank $I^*$ at about $0.81$ zero-shot (`GAT-P-QoS` $0.806$, `GAT-QoS` $0.805$), far above closed-form centrality ($0.511$–$0.526$), but their active-stratum agreement is weak ($\rho_{>0} \le 0.342$). The reference rankings reach higher (transitive dependents $0.938$, $\rho_{>0} = 0.871$; direct dependents $0.863$), because on these models most of $I^*$ is reachability itself. The models differ structurally from the synthetic corpus, and all five were written by one author, so these numbers describe transfer to those models, not to deployed systems. The centrality prior costs transfer: both hybrids fall below their base learners ($0.695$ and $0.662$ against $0.760$ and $0.805$).*

Table 11 replaces two comparisons earlier revisions got wrong. They set the full detection gate (system-layer analysis plus 18 anti-pattern detectors) against the five-seed labeling sweep over three node types and called that sweep “one run” of $I^*$ (median $5.6\times$; Supplementary Table S26), and they read Table 12 against Table 13, which were timed in different sessions on different graphs.
