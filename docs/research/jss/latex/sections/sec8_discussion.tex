\section{Discussion, Threats to Validity, and Conclusion}
\label{sec:8}

\subsection{Discussion and Practical Implications}
\label{sec:8.1}

\paragraph{Role of the Predictive Pathway}
Heterogeneous graph learning proves superior in three critical aspects:
(1)~\emph{Zero-Shot Inductive Generalization:} It is the most effective \emph{learned} model on unfamiliar architectures ($\rho = 0.664$ out-of-distribution); at matched QoS encoding the typed model leads its untyped counterpart by $+0.082$, and by $+0.064$ when neither encodes QoS, in both cases winning all twelve folds ($p = 0.0005$; Section~\ref{sec:7.2}). The margin is small --- a third of Topo-QoS's own $+0.253$ over unweighted centrality --- but it is the only comparison in this study that wins every fold, under both edge channels and in both evaluation regimes.
(2)~\emph{Shortlist Identification:} It attains the highest critical-set score of any variant we evaluated ($F_1@K = 0.439$), though its margin over the training-free QoS baseline is small and not statistically significant (7/12 folds, $p = 0.23$; Section~\ref{sec:7.1}), and it does not separate from untyped learning on this metric at all ($-0.001$, $p = 0.32$).
(3)~\emph{Low Marginal Cost:} Re-scoring an analysed graph costs $43.7\,\text{ms}$, and the complete gate including feature computation runs in $0.02$--$27.4\,\text{s}$ --- fast enough for pull-request gating rather than deferred nightly batch sweeps (Section~\ref{sec:7.5}).
Beyond scalar rankings, HGT provides relation-specific attention over the channels driving cascade spread, per-relationship edge criticality, and multi-task quality attributions that degree centralities cannot produce.

\paragraph{Boundary Conditions and When Not to Train}
The honest comparison is against a training-free QoS-weighted centrality baseline (\texttt{Topo-QoS}). On the synthetic folds the out-of-distribution ranking margin is $+0.058$ at fixed configuration ($p = 0.204$, Table~\ref{tab:7}) and $+0.115$ under nested selection ($p = 0.052$, Section~\ref{sec:7.1.nested}) --- neither reaching significance.

Off the corpus the result is not a tie but a loss. On the five open-source reference systems, scored against the oracle the model was trained on and against training-free references computed from the identical labels and node sets, HGT-QoS averages $\rho = 0.125$ where RM reaches $0.516$ and \texttt{Topo} $0.511$, going negative on two of the five (Section~\ref{sec:7.4.learned}). This is the case the learned model was supposed to own --- architectures structurally unlike anything in training --- and it is the case where the baseline wins by the largest margin in the study. We take the two results together rather than separately: a learned ranker that does not separate from a closed-form structural score in distribution, and falls well behind it out of distribution, is not justified by ranking accuracy on this evidence.

The recommendation follows directly. A team that needs a component ranking should use \texttt{Topo-QoS}: no training, no corpus, no checkpoints, and better transfer to architectures it has never seen. Training is justified by what the baseline structurally cannot produce --- relational attention over the channels carrying a cascade, per-relationship edge criticality, and multi-task quality attributions --- and by one property the learned model does deliver on real systems: it signals its own unreliability. The $\hat{\sigma}$ diagnostic of Section~\ref{sec:7.2.3}, derived on synthetic folds, correctly rejects all five open-source architectures at a threshold calibrated on the corpus (Section~\ref{sec:7.4.learned}). A predictor that can be trusted to say when it should not be trusted is deployable in a gate; one that is merely accurate on average is not. That, rather than a ranking margin, is where we would locate the learned pathway's contribution on present evidence.

The continuous QoS encodings should be read in similarly measured terms. On the pre-repair corpus they did not improve ranking accuracy ($-0.005$, $p = 0.68$) but halved seed-to-seed variance, which matters for a gate whose verdict should not depend on an initialisation. Whether the accuracy result survives the corpus repair of Section~\ref{sec:7.provenance} is untested.

\paragraph{Role of the Explanation Layer}
The RM profile separates single-point-of-failure exposure (Availability) from wide error propagation (Fault Tolerance), a distinction neither the predictor nor the oracle produces and one that decides between replication and circuit-breaking. Table~\ref{tab:7} and Figure~\ref{fig:5} therefore list RM alongside the ranking predictors as a diagnostic reference, not a competitor. Its practical value rests on that distinction being correct and useful, which this study specifies but does not evaluate against expert judgement or remediation outcomes.

\subsection{Performance and Computational Sustainability Implications}
\label{sec:8.2}

The cost profile in Section~\ref{sec:7.5} inverts the usual intuition about deep learning in continuous delivery: within SaG the neural model is the cheapest stage, and its margin widens with scale ($12\times$ at 249 nodes, $545\times$ at 1{,}998). Because feature extraction is a prerequisite rather than an alternative, the figure that matters operationally is the end-to-end one, and it is small enough for per-commit gating without a simulation cluster. Two qualifications bound this. First, the deterministic stage is $O(|V|^2 + |V||E|)$, so the corpus measurements do not extrapolate to the very large deployments the introduction invokes: at the observed growth, a $10^4$-component system would leave the pull-request budget, and we have not measured one. Second, the quantity we measured is wall-clock time, not energy. No power counters were instrumented, the mapping from CPU-seconds to joules depends on hardware and utilisation we did not control, and a latency ratio is not an energy ratio.

\paragraph{On the sustainability argument} The broader claim available to a framework of this kind --- that preventing cascading failures averts the retry storms, restart thrashing and connection-pool starvation that burn production capacity on uncompleted work --- is a motivation for the research programme, not a result of this study. We did not deploy SaG, prevent an incident, or measure avoided compute. We state the mechanism because it is what would make the work matter for sustainability, and we mark it as unmeasured because it is.

\subsection{Threats to Validity}
\label{sec:8.3}

\paragraph{Construct Validity} Our ground truth is generated via discrete-event cascade simulation on structural models rather than observing live production outages. To characterize construct divergence, we evaluated the three reliability-facing simulation engines (\texttt{FaultInjector}, \texttt{FailureSimulator}, \texttt{MessageFlowSimulator}) of our four-oracle taxonomy (Section~\ref{sec:4.3}). On \emph{ordering}, the evidence converges strongly: the behavioural queue-flow simulator and the topological cascade oracle agree at $\rho = 0.907$ (never below $0.748$ across scenarios), confirming that $I^*(v)$ tracks dynamic service disruption reach rather than purely static connectivity. However, queue simulation deltas are unweighted by message priority and only partially sensitive to delivery policies. On \emph{critical-set membership}, agreement is lower: top-$K$ Jaccard sits between $0.24$ and $0.49$ (against $0.111$ by chance). Tie-handling accounts for part of this and we no longer claim otherwise: on the regenerated corpus the tie-robust cut shifts the mean Jaccard by up to $0.023$ and a single scenario by as much as $0.167$, where an earlier draft reported the shift as negligible. The residual reflects intrinsic non-linear cascade threshold sensitivity and oracle self-agreement noise (oracles achieve only $0.44$ Jaccard against their own re-runs in worst-case scenarios). The related zero-inflation bound is reported in Section~\ref{sec:7.5.1}: $I^*(v)$ ties $19$--$106$ Applications per scenario at exactly zero, and restricting agreement to components both oracles score positive moves individual scenarios by $0.2$--$0.3$ in \emph{both} directions --- including \texttt{hub\_and\_spoke}, whose apparent sign disagreement ($\rho = -0.044$) becomes $\rho_{>0} = +0.263$ and is therefore a tie artifact rather than inverted ordering. A further 30--47\% of components per scenario previously carried no simulated ground truth because the cascade model could not express direct Topic or Node failure; the injector now expresses both (a failed Topic loses its whole feed regardless of surviving publishers, and a failed host takes every component deployed on it via \texttt{RUNS\_ON} simultaneously), and both strata are fully labelled and non-degenerate across all seven scenarios. The extension is additive --- neither branch is reachable from an Application, Broker or Library injection --- so every label those strata were scored on is unchanged and no table in Section~\ref{sec:7} moves. Coverage is not informativeness, however, and we report each new stratum against the count baseline that would explain it away (subscriber fan-out for Topic, colocation count for Node): Spearman $\rho$ against that baseline ranges $-0.05$--$0.70$ for Topic and $0.15$--$0.78$ for Node, so the strata are not restatements of a degree count, but the comparison is reported rather than omitted. No learned model is trained on either stratum, so the typing result of Section~\ref{sec:7.2} does not extend to them. The fourth oracle, $I_M(v)$ (\texttt{ChangePropagationSimulator}), targets Maintainability and traverses the same derived dependency topology from which $M(v)$ is scored; agreement between them serves as an internal consistency check rather than independent validation. Settling this requires an external referent independent of topology, such as version-control code churn or historical co-change coupling, left to future work.

\paragraph{Internal Validity and Substrate Confound} Potential feature leakage is prevented by strict graph view separation: predictors operate exclusively on $G_{\text{analysis}}$, whereas ground-truth simulators operate on $G_{\text{structural}}$. Two design changes made during development bear on how the shipped formulation should be read. First, an early hard articulation-point gate caused Availability $A(v)$ to collapse to a constant multiple of $w(v)$ in six of the eight scenarios then in the corpus where applications do not form cut vertices; replacing it with a continuous redundancy-deficit metric (fractional increase in average shortest paths upon removal) restored structural discriminability, and Equation~(\ref{eq:availability}) is the post-change form. That diagnosis was made on the pre-recalibration code and, unlike every measurement in Section~\ref{sec:7}, is not backed by a regenerable artifact in the replication package; we record it as development history, not as a result. Second, we identified and resolved a normalization defect in the code-quality scoring pipeline: a min-max normalization helper previously assigned a maximal penalty to zero-variance populations, penalizing unanalyzed libraries lacking source-level \texttt{code\_metrics}. Scoring undifferentiated populations with zero penalty corrected library Maintainability tiers while leaving Application rank correlations unaffected ($\Delta\rho \in \{-0.003, 0.000, 0.000\}$). In addition, the corpus on which Sections~\ref{sec:7.1}--\ref{sec:7.3} were measured assigned an identical \texttt{(RELIABLE, TRANSIENT\_LOCAL)} pair to every topic in seven of its twelve scenarios, giving Shannon entropy of exactly zero on all three QoS fields and leaving $w(t)$ standard deviation $\approx 0.02$ there. \texttt{Topo-QoS} nevertheless gained $+0.319\,\rho$ over \texttt{Topo} in those scenarios (Table~\ref{tab:5}), a gain resulting from uniform edge rescaling and from \texttt{Topic.criticality}, which remained non-degenerate everywhere, rather than from QoS diversity. That defect has since been traced to the generator reading a name-pattern lookup instead of the declared per-scenario distributions, and repaired; the corpus that ships with this paper has \emph{zero} degenerate scenarios of twelve (Section~\ref{sec:7.provenance}). Results predating the repair are marked in their captions, and their QoS-related readings are provisional until re-measured --- including the ablation of Section~\ref{sec:7.3.qos}, whose null result is what an inert treatment produces and is not evidence that declared QoS is uninformative. Independently of the repair, three of the seven QoS edge dimensions (\texttt{has\_deadline}, \texttt{deadline\_ns\_log}, \texttt{max\_blocking\_ms\_log}) remain identically zero in all twelve scenarios, because \texttt{QoSPolicy} carries no such fields for the generator to emit; populating them is a precondition for testing the per-dimension encoding claim at all, and we flag it as required future work rather than as a settled negative finding. As established in Section~\ref{sec:6.2.substrate}, both learned models operate on the shared native multigraph in both evaluations, so the out-of-distribution margin carries no multi-entity visibility confound. Three further confounds \emph{were} present in an earlier version of the harness and are quantified in Section~\ref{sec:7.2.1}: the homogeneous branch trained on one scenario rather than $N-1$, depth varied with which scenario was held out, and checkpoints were selected on a split of a training graph. Correcting them moves the untyped baseline from $\rho = 0.115$ to $0.600$ and reduces the typed margin from $+0.246$ to $+0.082$. We report the corrected figure throughout and retain the attribution table so that the discrepancy against the shipped replication package is explicable rather than mysterious. The residual margin still combines two factors --- GAT-N-QoS's edge channel carries only the scalar aggregate $w(e)$ whereas HGT-QoS consumes all 16 dimensions --- but the QoS ablation of Section~\ref{sec:7.3.qos} now attributes \emph{none} of it to the QoS dimensions ($-0.005$, $p = 0.68$), leaving typing itself as the operative factor. Contrary to what we previously reported, the typed advantage is not confined to distribution shift: it is present in-distribution as well ($+0.088$, 6/7, $p = 0.047$), so relational typing should be understood as a consistent small benefit in both regimes rather than as a purely inductive bias. Furthermore, as disclosed in Section~\ref{sec:8.2}, sustainability claims rest on CPU latency reductions rather than direct hardware power counters (RAPL, NVML).

\paragraph{External Validity and Real-World Metric Stratification} While our evaluation spans twelve architectures across eight domains, including five authentic open-source distributed systems (ROS~2 Autoware, Cloud Microservices, Train-Ticket, Home Assistant, EdgeX Foundry), future work should evaluate larger enterprise deployments with hundreds of microservices. Crucially, as shown in Table~\ref{tab:9}, evaluating top-$K$ critical sets across the pooled multigraph yields elevated $F_1@K$ scores ($0.667$--$1.000$) because unmeasured infrastructure entities carry constant zero failure labels. Reporting stratified metrics on active application services ($F_1@K = 0.000$--$0.625$) provides the true, non-inflated operational baseline.

\paragraph{Conclusion Validity} Given the heavy-tailed distribution of cascading failure impacts, statistical comparisons utilize non-parametric rank correlation (Spearman $\rho$, Kendall $\tau$), bootstrap confidence intervals ($B = 2,000$), and paired Wilcoxon signed-rank tests. Two critical hazards that altered conclusions in this study deserve explicit statement:
(1)~\emph{Simpson's Paradox:} Pooling rank correlations across node types moves them outside the envelope of the per-type correlations they aggregate, on both benchmarks we ran. Against $I_{\text{comp}}(v)$, RM's per-type correlations of $0.503$/$0.395$/$0.142$ pool to $0.028$ (Section~\ref{sec:7.3}). Against $I^*(v)$ on the LOSO folds, pooling places RM at $-0.014$ and untyped GAT-N at $0.381$, exactly reversing the standing that stratified evaluation gives them ($+0.195$ and $0.086$). The two pooled figures are separate measurements against different oracles and corpora, and are not comparable to each other. All reported metrics are therefore stratified on a single stated population ($V_{\text{app}}$).
(2)~\emph{Substrate Conflation:} Early RM ablations scored $Q(v)$ from features restricted to the Application--Library \texttt{DEPENDS\_ON} projection, where cut-vertex Availability terms vanished, producing false negative rank correlations. Computing features through the full multigraph pipeline resolved this issue.

\subsection{Limitations and Future Work}
\label{sec:8.4}

\paragraph{Distributed AI and LLM Serving Topologies} Modern AI infrastructure relies on distributed LLM serving systems (e.g., vLLM, Triton, DeepSpeed) characterized by complex tensor and pipeline parallelism across GPU nodes, dynamic KV-cache routing, and disaggregated prefill-decode architectures. A straggler or failing node in a pipeline-parallel ring induces severe head-of-line blocking and massive GPU idle power dissipation. Extending the SaG multigraph schema to model distributed model serving topologies (nodes representing GPU workers, edges encoding tensor communication channels and KV-cache transfer fabrics) offers a promising avenue to forecast and mitigate bottlenecks in AI serving clusters.

\paragraph{Empirical Power and Hardware Testbeds} Validating the sustainability thesis through empirical power measurements using running package counters (Intel/AMD RAPL, NVIDIA NVML) and IPMI sensors on Kubernetes clusters during live fault injection, alongside Hardware-in-the-Loop (HIL) validation on cyber-physical testbeds (e.g., ROS~2 CAN-bus autonomous driving compute boxes).

\paragraph{Automated Refactoring and Self-Healing} Extending SaG from predictive analysis to prescriptive synthesis---automatically generating pull requests that reconfigure QoS policies, insert circuit-breakers, and add redundant broker pathways to eliminate single points of failure.

\subsection{Conclusion}
\label{sec:8.5}

This work introduced \textbf{Software-as-a-Graph (SaG)}, a pre-deployment Static System Analysis framework that bridges the Architecture--Code Gap by combining a relation-specific Heterogeneous Graph Transformer (HGT) for failure-impact forecasting with an interpretable ISO/IEC 25010 Reliability--Maintainability explanation layer. Of the three concerns the framework is motivated by, this study delivers evidence on one and a half: \textbf{reliability}, through inductive cross-topology failure blast-radius forecasting on unseen architectures, and \textbf{analysis cost}, through a complete pre-deployment gate measured in seconds rather than cluster-hours. \textbf{Performance} and \textbf{energy} are motivating concerns we do not measure --- no latency or throughput quantity is predicted, and no power counter was instrumented.

Our empirical results across synthetic systems and five open-source reference systems (ROS~2 Autoware, Cloud Microservices, Train-Ticket, Home Assistant, EdgeX Foundry) show that typed learning over the full multigraph generalizes out of distribution better than untyped learning at matched training set, depth and model selection: $\rho = 0.664$ vs.\ $0.600$ without QoS edge features and $0.659$ vs.\ $0.577$ with them, 12 of 12 folds in both cases ($p = 0.0005$). In-distribution the same ordering holds at $+0.088$ (6/7, $p = 0.047$), so typing is a small consistent benefit in both regimes rather than a purely inductive bias. Because every fold in this comparison is drawn from a single parameterized generator, the typing claim is established on synthetic architectures alone. Tested off the corpus it does not hold up: on five open-source reference systems the trained model averages $\rho = 0.125$ against $0.516$ and $0.511$ for two training-free references scored on identical labels, going negative on two of the five (Section~\ref{sec:7.4.learned}). What does transfer is the model's own reliability signal --- the label-free $\hat{\sigma}$ diagnostic correctly rejects all five. We are equally explicit about the boundary: against an unparameterized QoS-weighted centrality baseline (\texttt{Topo-QoS}), out-of-distribution ranking is not beaten --- $\Delta\rho = +0.058$ ($p = 0.204$) at fixed configuration and $+0.115$ ($p = 0.052$) under nested selection --- and the critical-set margin ($F_1@K = 0.439$ vs.\ $0.375$) is not significant either (7 of 12 folds, $p = 0.23$). On present evidence a team needing only a ranking should use the baseline, in distribution and out of it. The case for the learned model rests on what the baseline cannot produce --- relational attention, per-relationship criticality, standards-grounded attribution --- and on its capacity to flag the architectures where it should not be trusted, which is the property that makes a pre-deployment gate safe to automate. Quantifying the first three is the natural next step; so is re-measuring Sections~\ref{sec:7.1}--\ref{sec:7.3} on the repaired corpus (Section~\ref{sec:7.provenance}). SaG establishes the typed substrate on which both prediction and explanation can be posed, providing an empirical and architectural foundation for dependable distributed systems engineering.
