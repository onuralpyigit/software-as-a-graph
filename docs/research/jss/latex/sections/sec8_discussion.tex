\section{Discussion, Threats to Validity, and Limitations}
\label{sec:8}

\subsection{Practical Consequences}
\label{sec:8.1}

\noindent\textbf{The representation carries the signal.} The most robust result is that SaG's QoS-aware dependency projection makes criticality legible to simple and learned analyzers alike. Closed-form centrality on the projection improves every held-out architecture over its unweighted form ($+0.204$). For learned engines, the QoS inputs are what matter ($+0.073$ at matched capacity), through declared-coupling node features rather than the edge channel. Practitioners therefore gain most from modeling their architecture with typed entities and declared QoS contracts, whichever engine they then run. On that representation, closed-form and learned engines fail on different architectures, so combining them is worth more than choosing between them.

\noindent\textbf{Choosing an engine.} Table~\ref{tab:guidance} summarizes where each instrument fits. The closed-form engine needs no training and is the natural default for lightweight CI gates. The hybrids are the most accurate choice for architectures resembling the training corpus: they keep the closed-form engine's strength where it ranks well, as on Enterprise, while adding the learned engines' gains elsewhere. Hybrid-HGT is the registered recommendation because it transfers better of the two. For substantially different systems, a pure learned engine transfers best; because relation-specific weights add nothing at matched capacity, the untyped \texttt{GAT-QoS} is the simpler choice. The QoS inputs that help in distribution are not what transfers: the untyped \texttt{GAT} without them reaches $0.831$ zero-shot (Section~\ref{sec:8.2}). The explanation layer then names a remediation class for each flagged component.

\noindent\textbf{Computational sustainability.} Pre-deployment analysis avoids provisioning staging clusters for chaos sweeps \cite{calero2015green,verdecchia2023greenai}; we state this as infrastructure avoidance, not a measured energy saving. At base SoC power ($28\,\text{W}$), one pass of the analysis gate over all twelve scenarios costs at most $0.83\,\text{Wh}$, and training the four learned arms once costs about $0.22\,\text{kWh}$, both upper bounds from wall-clock time. Direct RAPL/NVML measurement \cite{lannelongue2021green,schmidt2021codecarbon} and incremental caching of structural metrics across commits are the main open levers.

\noindent\textbf{Where learned engines help.} Learned engines are steady across architectures and the closed-form engine is not: by tercile, \texttt{HGT-QoS} scores $0.604$, $0.589$ and $0.672$, whereas \texttt{Topo-QoS} scores $0.324$, $0.561$ and $0.775$. Which engine wins on an architecture is therefore decided by how well centrality happens to fit it. Learned engines win every fold where centrality ranks poorly, such as the Microservices and ATM meshes, and on topologies whose symmetric stars create betweenness ties (on EdgeX, closed-form Overlap@$K$ is $0$). They are also the only engines that stay reliable on architectures unlike their training corpus. Because the terciles are defined with the labels, no architecture can be placed in one before deployment, and no descriptor we measured predicts it (see below). The hybrid is therefore the safe default in distribution. It matches or beats both of its components in two terciles, and it gives up $0.07$--$0.10$ against its learned engine where centrality is weakest.

\noindent\textbf{What succeeds is learning over graph-derived features.} Three controls locate the learned engines' accuracy. First, gradient-boosted trees over the same per-node features, with no graph (\texttt{GBM-Feat}, an exploratory baseline since Amendment~3), reach $\rho = 0.642$ under LOSO, level with \texttt{GAT-QoS} ($0.635$). Second, every relation on the native substrate points away from Applications, so the GATs, which pass messages along edge direction, never deliver one to the population they score: deleting every edge leaves their Application outputs unchanged. Third, HGT reaches Applications only through its reverse pass, and removing it costs nothing (\texttt{HGT-QoS-U}, Section~\ref{sec:rq2}). The learned engines thus succeed as learned combiners of the graph-derived node features of Section~\ref{sec:3.4}, such as centralities, the Connectivity Degradation Index and QoS coupling weights, rather than through relational reasoning at inference. The neural models add value over trees in transfer: zero-shot, \texttt{GAT-QoS} reaches $\rho = 0.805$ and $\rho_{>0} = 0.319$, against $0.757$ and $0.156$ for \texttt{GBM-Feat}. In distribution, trees lead on the active stratum ($0.391$ vs.\ $0.338$). Claims for message passing on similar tasks should be checked against such a feature-only learner.

\noindent\textbf{Why learning fails where it fails.} (i)~The target is nearly topological: a topology-only relabeling recovers $I^*$'s ordering at $\rho = 0.965$ (Section~\ref{sec:4.3}), and the features already contain the centralities that approximate it. Where centrality ranks well, a learned fit to eleven other architectures replaces a near-sufficient statistic and loses, most on Enterprise ($0.426$ vs.\ $0.795$). (ii)~Training support is narrow: twelve graphs from one generator, and Enterprise, with 300 applications against at most 200 elsewhere, lies outside it. (iii)~RPC failure semantics are absent: neither the features nor the forward-propagating oracle encodes synchronous calls, so no engine reliably orders the active components of the RPC-derived models. (iv)~Between 21\% and 52\% of each population is inert, so much of any full-population $\rho$ separates harmless from harmful components rather than ordering them (Section~\ref{sec:rq1}). Receptive-field size is \emph{not} a reason: the share of the graph from which HGT reaches an Application ($35$--$59\%$ per fold) is uncorrelated with its per-fold $\rho$ (Spearman $+0.12$) or with its gain over \texttt{Topo-QoS} ($-0.06$).

\noindent\textbf{Homogeneous or heterogeneous?} On this evidence no scenario class calls for a heterogeneous model. At matched capacity, typing adds nothing on average ($-0.014$). \texttt{HGT-QoS} wins 4 of 12 folds against \texttt{GAT-QoS} (ATM, Enterprise, Logistics Fleet, Real-Time Gaming), and these share no descriptor we measured. The untyped engine also transfers better on all five system models. One exploratory pattern deserves a test. Without QoS inputs, HGT's margin over GAT grows with scale: across folds, its Spearman correlation is $+0.63$ with the number of applications, $+0.66$ with topics and $+0.77$ with brokers (Enterprise $+0.122$, Healthcare $-0.131$). The pattern vanishes once QoS inputs are present ($+0.03$ with applications). Typed parameters may therefore stand in for undeclared QoS contracts in large multi-broker systems. However, this is one of 96 descriptor--gain correlations, none of which survives a Benjamini--Hochberg correction ($q \ge 0.32$), so it is a hypothesis rather than a finding. The untyped engine is the default we recommend. On scale more generally, across folds of 26 to 300 applications, the learned engines' gain over \texttt{Topo-QoS} falls with size (Spearman $-0.48$ with applications, $-0.62$ with libraries), while the hybrid's correction gains value ($+0.43$), again without surviving correction. Ranking accuracy was not measured above 300 applications; the 2,000-component rows of Table~\ref{tab:scale} measure cost only.

\subsection{Threats to Validity}
\label{sec:threats}

\noindent\textbf{Construct validity.} All labels are simulator-derived rather than observed failures. The behavioral queue-flow oracle agrees with the primary cascade oracle at $\rho = 0.627$ (Section~\ref{sec:4.3}); much of that agreement concerns which components are harmless. Because $I^*(v)$ is a reachability functional of the topology the predictors read, a strong closed-form comparator is expected. Retargeting the LOSO contrasts on $I_{\text{dyn}}$ is the next experiment. The five system models are the most judgement-laden input: each was written by one author, no second modeler has re-derived them, and RQ3's figures hold only for these models. The replication package includes a re-modeling protocol and an agreement tool (\path{reproduce/model_agreement.py}) so that this check is cheap to run.

\noindent\textbf{Internal validity.} Predictors consume $G_{\text{analysis}}$ and oracles traverse $G_{\text{structural}}$, which is enforced in CI. Substrate, training set, depth and early stopping are matched across learned arms. All typing conclusions rest on the capacity- and channel-matched control, and all four registered control arms have run, so capacity, edge-channel width and message directionality are each matched (Section~\ref{sec:8.2}). The regime analysis of Section~\ref{sec:8.2} is post hoc. No hyperparameter was tuned on an evaluation split.

\noindent\textbf{Robustness to free parameters.} Under Morris screening only two of ten declared constants, the AHP shrinkage $\lambda$ and $r_{\text{FT}}$, have appreciable influence ($\mu^* \approx 0.13$ against $\le 0.025$ for the rest), and no setting of the topic-weight or QoS sub-weight constants changes any reported comparison (Supplementary Sections~\ref{S-supp:params}--\ref{S-supp:ahp}).

\noindent\textbf{External validity.} The synthetic corpus comes from one generator family, and the system models are small (22--41 applications). Scaling beyond 2,000 nodes would benefit from incremental caching or mini-batching \cite{zeng2020graphsaint}.

\noindent\textbf{Conclusion validity and repeatability.} LOSO folds share training scenarios, so $p$-values are nominal \cite{nadeau2003inference} and are read alongside fold-level sign consistency and bootstrap intervals. At fixed code, seeds and device, every figure reproduces at its reported precision, and all training-free cells also reproduce across devices. Learned cells move across code revisions and devices (up to $0.172$ in a fold mean; \texttt{HGT-QoS} $0.041$), so learned figures are reported against the released artifacts, and every comparison is made within one sweep. The drift ledger is in the replication repository (\path{reproduce/rerun_drift.py}).

\subsection{Limitations and Future Work}
\label{sec:limitations}

The explanation layer's attributions have not been evaluated with developers or against injected faults, and no published learned-criticality model (FINDER \cite{fan2020keyplayers}, DrBC \cite{fan2019learning}) has been reproduced on this corpus. Next steps are: (1)~an independent re-model of at least two of the five systems, with inter-modeler agreement reported; (2)~combining the hybrids' in-distribution accuracy with the pure engines' transfer, for example by learning when to trust the prior; (3)~retargeting RQ1 and RQ2 on $I_{\text{dyn}}$; (4)~synchronous call edges and a backward-propagating oracle, so that RPC architectures can be modeled natively; (5)~extracting system models from deployment manifests; and (6)~validating rankings against production incident data.
