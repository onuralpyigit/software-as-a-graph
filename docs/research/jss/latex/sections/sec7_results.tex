\subsection{RQ1: Ranking Accuracy Against Structural Baselines}
\label{sec:rq1}
\label{sec:rq1-hybrid}
\label{sec:rq1-loso}

\paragraph{Summary} \emph{Counting a component's direct dependents (\texttt{InDeg}), which is publish--subscribe afferent coupling, ranks $I^*$ at $\rho = 0.764$ and beats closed-form centrality (\texttt{Topo-QoS}, $0.553$) on all twelve synthetic architectures ($+0.211$, Holm $p = 0.002$, exploratory). It is computable on the raw multigraph as a typed two-hop query; untyped raw-graph metrics fall far below it, but simply counting the topics a component publishes comes within $0.033$. A dependency-graph GAT matches it ($0.748$) without exceeding it. On $I_{\text{dyn}}$ the count keeps a partial correlation of $0.259$ after the rank of $I^*$ is removed; on $I_{\text{comp}}$ it loses to QoS-weighted centrality. Recovering $80\%$ of the true top-$20\%$ set requires flagging the top $45\%$ by \texttt{InDeg}.}

Learned engines are trained on eleven scenarios and scored on the twelfth; training-free rankers are simply scored on each of the twelve (26 to 300 Applications, $K$ between 5 and 60). Table~\ref{tab:hybrid} reports all of them against $I^*$.

\noindent\textbf{The count, and what the derivation adds to it.} \texttt{InDeg} reaches $\rho = 0.764$ ($\rho_{>0} = 0.516$) and beats \texttt{Topo-QoS} on every fold; \texttt{Reach}, the number of transitive dependents, reaches $0.732$ (11/12). By Proposition~1, \texttt{InDeg} is publish--subscribe afferent coupling \cite{martin2003agile,rud2006ais,bogner2017maintainability} and can be computed on the raw multigraph as a typed two-hop query. The raw-graph rankers of Table~\ref{tab:hybrid} separate what the choice of that query contributes from what the derived graph contributes. Untyped raw-graph metrics fail: total degree reaches $0.199$ and reverse PageRank $0.089$, and PageRank is constant for every Application because Applications have no incoming raw edges (\texttt{InDeg} ahead on 12/12 folds, Holm $p = 0.006$ each). A trivial typed count does not fail: the number of topics a component publishes (Pubs-raw) reaches $0.731$, only $0.033$ below \texttt{InDeg} (11/12 folds, Holm $p = 0.002$; Amendment~10, corrected in this revision, Supplementary Section~\ref{S-supp:amendments}). \textbf{The $+0.211$ margin over centrality is therefore a property of choosing afferent coupling as the metric, not of the dependency projection.} What the projection adds beyond the typed query is measured in one place: without Rule~5, \texttt{Reach} falls by $0.058$ (9/12 folds, Holm $p = 0.0068$; Amendment~10).

\noindent\textbf{Learners on the dependency graph match the count.} The same graph neural networks, trained on the dependency graph instead of the raw multigraph, reach $\rho = 0.748$ (\texttt{GAT-P-QoS}), level with \texttt{InDeg} ($-0.017$, Holm $p = 1.000$). Adding the \texttt{InDeg} score as a prior yields $0.758$ (\texttt{GAT-P+InDeg}): learning a correction to the count adds nothing measurable to it. Every learner already receives in-degree as a node feature (Section~\ref{sec:3.5}); a learner without that feature was not run, so whether message passing alone would recover the count is untested. \texttt{HGT-P-QoS} reaches only $0.514$ and is highly seed-dependent (mean within-fold seed SD $0.254$; Section~\ref{sec:8.3}).

\noindent\textbf{Hybrids outperform closed-form centrality, not the count.} Hybrid-HGT reaches $\rho = 0.657$ ($+0.103$, Holm $p = 0.0068$) and Hybrid-GAT reaches $\rho = 0.683$ ($+0.130$, Holm $p = 0.0029$), each on 11 of 12 folds. Both survive omnibus Holm correction across all thirteen confirmatory contrasts ($p_{\text{omni}} = 0.041$ and $0.019$). These are the study's only significant confirmatory results. On their own, the raw-multigraph learned engines do not differ significantly from \texttt{Topo-QoS} (\texttt{HGT-QoS} $+0.069$, $p = 0.266$, the null primary contrast; \texttt{GAT-QoS} $+0.082$, $p = 0.233$), and both hybrids rank below \texttt{InDeg} ($0.657$ and $0.683$ against $0.764$).

\noindent\textbf{Where the closed-form gain comes from.} \texttt{Topo} reads betweenness from the application-layer graph, whereas \texttt{Topo-QoS} computes QoS-weighted betweenness on the Application--Library dependency graph. Three controls registered in Amendment~7 separate substrate from weighting: unweighted betweenness on the same graph scores $0.591$; constant topic weights score $0.595$; permuting QoS profiles scores $0.559$ ($-0.006$, $p = 0.73$). On $I^*$, the gain comes from the Application--Library graph structure, not from QoS contract content.

\noindent\textbf{The first-order expansion of the oracle.} To show how much of \texttt{InDeg}'s agreement with $I^*$ comes from the simulator's own propagation rule, Table~\ref{tab:hybrid} includes a post hoc closed-form first-order expansion of $I^*$:
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:analytic_istar}
\hat{I}^*_1(v) = \sum_{t \in \text{pub}(v)} \frac{|\text{sub}(t)|}{|\text{pub}(t)|},
\end{equation}
where $|\text{pub}(t)| \ge 1$ holds for all published topics $t \in \text{pub}(v)$ by construction ($v$ publishes to $t$), preventing any division by zero. It reaches mean $\rho = 0.808$ $[0.755, 0.853]$ ($\rho_{>0} = 0.631$): direct subscriber loss is most of what $I^*$ measures, and \texttt{InDeg} is a coarser version of the same quantity. As a reference it bounds the mean rather than every fold: on four folds (AV, Financial Trading, Healthcare, Industrial SCADA) \texttt{InDeg} slightly exceeds it (by $0.005$--$0.038$).

\noindent\textbf{Beyond the reachability oracle.} Table~\ref{tab:independent_oracles} scores the rankers against the two other oracles. $I_{\text{dyn}}$ agrees with $I^*$ only moderately ($\rho = 0.627$ $[0.495, 0.749]$ on the same sample), and its published test--retest reliability is $0.74$--$0.97$ (Supplementary Section~\ref{S-supp:convergent}), which bounds what any deterministic ranker can reach. On $I_{\text{dyn}}$, \texttt{InDeg} reaches $\rho = 0.610$ and beats \texttt{Topo-QoS} on 11 of 12 folds ($+0.217$, $p = 0.0034$). Because $I_{\text{dyn}}$ shares the topology and the first-order effect with $I^*$, raw agreement cannot show that the count captures anything the reachability oracle does not; the partial correlation can. After the rank of $I^*$ is removed, \texttt{InDeg} keeps $\rho = 0.259$ $[0.143, 0.367]$ with $I_{\text{dyn}}$, and $0.163$ $[0.043, 0.275]$ after the first-order expansion is removed instead; \texttt{Reach} keeps nothing ($0.058$, CI includes zero). The count therefore carries some queue-flow signal that the reachability oracle lacks, and transitive reach does not. Two caveats bound this: the sample is the lexicographic $30$ Applications per fold, and every learned engine is below \texttt{InDeg} on $I_{\text{dyn}}$ ($0.418$--$0.594$).

\noindent\textbf{The multi-criteria oracle favours different rankers.} On $I_{\text{comp}}$, which adds fragmentation, throughput and flow disruption (Section~\ref{sec:4.3}), QoS-weighted centrality ranks above the count ($\rho = 0.702$ $[0.649, 0.753]$ against $0.650$; \texttt{InDeg} ahead on only 3 of 12 folds, $p = 0.110$). Raw total degree, the weakest ranker on $I^*$, reaches $0.719$ on $I_{\text{comp}}$ (\texttt{InDeg} ahead on 4 of 12 folds, Holm $p = 0.220$), and \texttt{Reach} falls to $0.302$. The learned engines without a centrality prior collapse on this oracle ($0.144$--$0.334$); the two hybrids, which carry \texttt{Topo-QoS} as a prior, keep $0.58$. No ranker is best on all three oracles, so which one to use depends on which failure notion matters (Section~\ref{sec:8.1}).

\noindent\textbf{Identifying the critical set.} Rank correlation over all Applications is not the operational question in a CI gate, which is whether the true top of the ranking gets flagged. Figure~\ref{fig:recall} gives, for each ranker, the share of the true top-$20\%$ set on $I^*$ that falls within its top $k\%$, with ties resolved in expectation (Amendment~12). At $k = 20\%$, \texttt{InDeg} recovers $0.49$ of the critical set (tie-breaking bounds $0.48$--$0.50$), consistent with its Overlap@$K$ of $0.504$: about half of what a top-$20\%$ gate flags is not in the true top $20\%$, and about half of the true top $20\%$ is missed. Recovering $80\%$ of the critical set requires flagging the top $45\%$ by \texttt{InDeg} ($0.83$), the top $40\%$ by \texttt{GAT-P-QoS} ($0.81$) or the top $45\%$ by the first-order expansion ($0.89$); only the first-order expansion reaches $90\%$ within the top $50\%$, and \texttt{Reach} and \texttt{Topo-QoS} do not reach $80\%$ within $50\%$. On the $I_{\text{dyn}}$ sample, \texttt{InDeg} reaches $0.83$ at $40\%$ and \texttt{GAT-P-QoS} $0.65$.

\noindent\textbf{The ``QoS'' factor is confounded with the count.} Its main effect ($+0.073$) is not significant after Holm correction ($p_{\text{Holm}} = 0.127$), and it could not be attributed to QoS content even if it were. In the node features, $w_{\text{in}}$ is the sum of incoming dependency weights, a QoS-weighted version of \texttt{InDeg}; the QoS-off arms zero it, and so remove the single most predictive quantity in the study along with the QoS contracts. A rerun of the $2\times2$ with $w_{\text{in}}$ kept in both arms was not performed. The closed-form controls point the same way: unweighted betweenness ($0.591$) and constant topic weights ($0.595$) match or exceed QoS-weighted betweenness ($0.553$), and permuting QoS profiles across topics changes nothing ($\Delta = -0.006$ $[-0.025, +0.015]$, $p = 0.733$). On $I^*$ we find no evidence that QoS contract content carries ranking signal.

\noindent\textbf{Relation typing under matched capacity.} \texttt{HGT-QoS} remains within $0.013$ of \texttt{GAT-QoS} (Table~\ref{tab:contrasts_matched}). On the raw multigraph this says little about typing, because no message reaches an Application there: a gradient-boosted model over the same per-node features without any graph (\texttt{GBM-Feat}, Amendment~8) reaches $0.642$, level with \texttt{GAT-QoS}, and removing HGT's reverse pass, the only route by which messages reach Applications, changes nothing (\texttt{HGT-QoS-U}, $-0.010$, $p = 0.91$; Supplementary Section~\ref{S-supp:amendments}). On the dependency graph, where messages do arrive, \texttt{HGT-P-QoS} reaches $0.514$ with a mean within-fold seed SD of $0.254$, against $0.748$ and $0.030$ for \texttt{GAT-P-QoS}. This is one untuned configuration (width 100, no hyperparameter search), and averaging its five seeds' predictions raises it to $0.618$; we therefore read it as seed instability of that configuration, not as evidence against relational typing in general.

\texttt{Reach} reaches $\rho = 0.938$ and $\rho_{>0} = 0.871$ on the five models: $0.836$--$0.997$ on the three originally publish--subscribe systems and $0.966$--$0.998$ on the two RPC systems re-expressed as pub-sub meshes (per system: Supplementary Table~\ref{S-tab:a7-systems}). These values exceed anything \texttt{Reach} reaches on the synthetic folds ($0.732$, $\rho_{>0} = 0.286$), and the difference is structural. Half of the system models' Applications have zero simulated impact ($0.51$ on average, against $0.31$ on the folds), and their fan-in is more concentrated (Gini $0.65$ against $0.50$; Supplementary Section~\ref{S-supp:referee}). On such graphs, separating the inert half from the rest already produces a high full-population $\rho$, and \texttt{Reach}, which is zero exactly for components with no dependents, does that perfectly. The active-stratum $\rho_{>0}$ is the more informative column here, and on it \texttt{Reach} ($0.871$) is far ahead of \texttt{InDeg} ($0.321$ $[-0.044, 0.687]$) and of every learned engine ($0.185$--$0.342$).
