\subsection{RQ2: Value of Typed Heterogeneity}
\label{sec:7.2}

To evaluate the contribution of node and edge typing, we compare relation-specific HGL against homogeneous GL across different validation regimes:
\begin{itemize}
    \item \textbf{In-Distribution:} Heterogeneous HGL leads homogeneous GL by $\Delta\rho = +0.020$ (0.730 vs.\ 0.710). On familiar topologies, homogeneous GNNs can approximate type distinctions through structural degree signatures.
    \item \textbf{Out-of-Distribution (LOSO):} Heterogeneous HGL outperforms homogeneous GL by \textbf{$+0.172$} ($\rho = 0.608$ vs.\ $0.436$) and exhibits substantially higher stability across folds. When encountering unseen topologies, relation-specific message passing is essential for generalizing failure mechanics.
\end{itemize}

\subsubsection{Empirical Edge-Removal Analysis}

We further probed edge criticality by simulating the removal of candidate relationship channels while keeping both endpoint components alive. On the \texttt{av\_system} topology across 50 candidate bridge edges, 46 edges exhibited zero downstream cascade impact, while the 4 edges with non-zero impact were direct library communication channels (\texttt{PUBLISHES\_TO} / \texttt{SUBSCRIBES\_TO}). This demonstrates that individual communication links are largely redundant, whereas component-level failures and shared library dependencies induce disproportionate systemic disruption.

\subsection{RQ3: Ablations and Sensitivity Analysis}
\label{sec:7.3}

\subsubsection{QoS Feature Encoding}
Explicitly encoding continuous QoS attributes in the GNN ($HGL\text{-}QoS$) yielded negligible differences compared to the base typed model (in-distribution: 0.731 vs.\ 0.730; LOSO: 0.595 vs.\ 0.608). The derived \texttt{DEPENDS\_ON} topology already embeds the primary routing and coupling effects, rendering explicit QoS edge features largely redundant when structural typing is available.

\subsubsection{Convergent Validity Across Simulation Oracles}

To test construct validity, we compared node criticality rankings across our three simulation engines: the topological cascade reachability oracle $I^*(v)$ (\texttt{FaultInjector}), the multi-metric composite oracle $I_{\text{comp}}(v)$ (\texttt{FailureSimulator}), and the discrete-event queue-flow simulator $I_{\text{dyn}}(v)$ (\texttt{MessageFlowSimulator}). 

Across all seven synthetic scenarios, the behavioural SimPy simulator ($I_{\text{dyn}}$) and the topological cascade reachability oracle ($I^*$) exhibited strong rank convergence:
\begin{itemize}
    \item \textbf{$I_{\text{dyn}}$ vs.\ $I^*(v)$:} Mean Spearman $\rho = \mathbf{0.7654}$ (ranging from $0.5485$ on Hub-and-Spoke to $0.9378$ on Healthcare), with mean Kendall $\tau = 0.630$ and top-$K$ Jaccard overlap of $0.316$.
    \item \textbf{$I_{\text{comp}}$ vs.\ $I_{\text{dyn}}$:} Mean Spearman $\rho = 0.4650$ (ranging from $0.1208$ to $0.6574$).
    \item \textbf{$I_{\text{comp}}$ vs.\ $I^*(v)$:} Mean Spearman $\rho = 0.3943$ (ranging from $0.0920$ to $0.5777$).
\end{itemize}
This strong agreement between independent behavioural queue simulations and structural cascade reachability confirms that our continuous target label $I^*(v)$ captures genuine dynamic service disruption.

\subsubsection{Domain-Specific Weighting Sensitivity}

We investigated the impact of Context of Use reweighting $\vec{\omega} = [q_R, q_M]^\top$ across 10 evaluation scenarios. Domain-specific reweighting $Q_{\text{domain}}(v)$ improved rank correlation by an average of $\Delta\rho = \mathbf{+0.0265}$ over static uniform weighting, maintaining an average Kendall rank fidelity of $\tau = 0.9677$. This demonstrates that operational Context of Use can be tuned without distorting underlying structural stability.

\subsubsection{Threshold and Normalization Sensitivity}

Sweeping 7 scenarios $\times$ 7 tier thresholds ($z \in [1.0, 2.5]$, IQR multipliers) $\times$ 3 normalization techniques (min-max, standard, rank-quantile) revealed that tier categorization is primarily sensitive to threshold selection ($\Delta\rho = 0.1896$ across extreme thresholds), whereas ranking performance is exceptionally stable across normalization regimes ($\Delta\rho = 0.0158$).

\subsubsection{Anti-Pattern Detection and CI/CD Quality Gates}

Validating our rule-based architectural anti-pattern catalog against the multi-metric composite oracle $I_{\text{comp}}(v)$ yielded a mean detection $F_1 = 0.3781$ across 8 system scenarios. Analysis execution times ranged from $0.04\,\text{s}$ (small scenarios, 50 nodes) to $54.85\,\text{s}$ (enterprise event meshes, 300 nodes), confirming that SaG runs comfortably within standard pre-commit and pull-request CI/CD quality gate budgets.

\subsubsection{HGT Attention Weight Analysis}

Extracting relational attention matrices from the trained HGT model on the ATM Case Study (Figure~3) revealed that the model places the highest attention weights ($\alpha_{uv} > 0.45$) on downstream \texttt{PUBLISHES\_TO} and \texttt{CALLS} dependencies connecting shared persistence brokers and authorization libraries, directly reflecting empirical cascade pathways.
