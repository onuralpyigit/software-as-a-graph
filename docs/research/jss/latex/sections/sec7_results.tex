\subsection{RQ1: Ranking Accuracy Against Structural Baselines}
\label{sec:rq1}
\label{sec:rq1-hybrid}
\label{sec:rq1-loso}

\paragraph{Summary} \emph{Against $I^*$, the strongest rankings are references that restate its first propagation wave (first-order expansion $0.808$, direct dependents $0.764$). The registered primary contrast, \texttt{HGT-QoS} against \texttt{Topo-QoS} ($0.553$), is null. The two hybrids beat \texttt{Topo-QoS} on 11 of 12 folds (Holm $p = 0.0068$ and $0.0029$), but not their own base learners, and only Hybrid-GAT beats unweighted betweenness after correction. The best GNN is a GAT on the dependency graph ($0.748$); it is statistically indistinguishable from the direct-dependent count, but not equivalent to it within $\pm 0.05$, and it depends on its degree features (Section~\ref{sec:rq2}). On full-population $I_{\text{dyn}}$ no GNN reaches the references, while a gradient-boosted surrogate trained on $I_{\text{dyn}}$ exceeds them; on $I_{\text{comp}}$ closed-form centrality and raw degree, which are proximate to its terms, rank highest. Recovering $80\%$ of the true top-$20\%$ set requires flagging the top $40\%$ by \texttt{GAT-P-QoS}.}

Learned engines are trained on eleven scenarios and scored on the twelfth; training-free rankers are simply scored on each of the twelve (26 to 300 Applications, $K$ between 5 and 60). \noindent\textbf{The reference level.} By Remark~1, \texttt{InDeg} is publish--subscribe afferent coupling \cite{martin2003agile,rud2006ais,bogner2017maintainability} and counts exactly the Applications that $I^*$'s first propagation wave reaches; \texttt{Reach} counts the transitive dependents a reachability cascade can visit. Their values on $I^*$ ($0.764$, $\rho_{>0} = 0.516$; $0.732$) therefore measure how much of the oracle a restatement of its rule recovers, not predictive skill, and the same holds for their raw-graph equivalents: counting the topics a component publishes (Pubs-raw) reaches $0.731$, and the raw publisher $\to$ topic $\to$ subscriber closure (Reach-R1) $0.674$. Among the references, the derivation contributes in one measured place: without Rule~5, \texttt{Reach} falls by $0.058$ (9/12 folds, Holm $p = 0.0068$; Amendment~10). Untyped raw-graph metrics recover none of the reference: total degree reaches $0.199$ and reverse PageRank $0.089$, and PageRank is constant for every Application because Applications have no incoming raw edges.

\noindent\textbf{Learners on the dependency graph approach the reference.} The same graph neural networks, trained on the dependency graph instead of the raw multigraph, reach $\rho = 0.748$ (\texttt{GAT-P-QoS}), the best GNN in Table~\ref{tab:hybrid}, and $0.016$ below the direct-dependent count on the per-seed statistic; its five-seed ensemble is $0.007$ above it ($0.772$, Table~\ref{tab:independent_oracles}). Neither difference is significant, and neither is equivalence: the $90\%$ interval of the per-fold difference reaches $\pm 0.076$, so a two one-sided test at the registered margin of $\pm 0.05$ fails ($p = 0.17$ per seed, $0.13$ for the ensemble; Amendment~14, F3). The learner and the count are therefore indistinguishable at this sample size, not ranked. The approach to the reference rests on the degree features every learner receives (Section~\ref{sec:3.5}): removing \texttt{in\_degree} and $w_{\text{in}}$ lowers \texttt{GAT-P-QoS} to $0.612$ (Section~\ref{sec:rq2}). A variant that uses \texttt{InDeg} as its prior is reported only in Supplementary Section~\ref{S-supp:amendment9}, because its prior is a reference. \texttt{HGT-P-QoS} reaches only $0.514$ and is highly seed-dependent (mean within-fold seed SD $0.254$; Section~\ref{sec:8.3}).

\noindent\textbf{Hybrids outperform the registered comparator, and little else.} Hybrid-HGT reaches $\rho = 0.657$ ($+0.103$, Holm $p = 0.0068$) and Hybrid-GAT $0.683$ ($+0.130$, Holm $p = 0.0029$) against \texttt{Topo-QoS}, each on 11 of 12 folds; both stay significant under the omnibus Holm correction ($p_{\text{omni}} = 0.041$ and $0.019$), under the Nadeau--Bengio corrected $t$ ($p = 0.019$ and $0.014$) and under $|V_{\text{app}}|$-weighted averaging (exact sign-flip $p = 0.038$ and $0.005$; Supplementary Section~\ref{S-supp:round8}). They are the study's only significant registered improvements, and they are weaker than they look, because a hybrid receives $p(v)$ as an input and adds a learned correction to its logit, so it nests its comparator. Against their own base learners, which Amendments~5 and~6 registered alongside, neither is significant (Hybrid-HGT vs.\ \texttt{HGT-QoS} $+0.035$, $p = 0.73$; Hybrid-GAT vs.\ \texttt{GAT-QoS} $+0.048$, $p = 0.30$). Against the two stronger training-free controls of Amendment~7 (Amendment~14, F7), Hybrid-GAT keeps a margin (unweighted betweenness on the same graph $+0.092$, constant topic weights $+0.088$, both Holm $p = 0.049$), Hybrid-HGT does not ($+0.065$ and $+0.061$, Holm $p = 0.085$). The prior also costs transfer: zero-shot, both hybrids fall below their base learners (Section~\ref{sec:rq3}). On their own, the raw-multigraph learned engines do not differ significantly from \texttt{Topo-QoS} (\texttt{HGT-QoS} $+0.069$, $p = 0.266$, the null primary contrast; \texttt{GAT-QoS} $+0.082$, $p = 0.233$).

\noindent\textbf{Where the closed-form gain comes from.} \texttt{Topo} reads betweenness from the application-layer graph, whereas \texttt{Topo-QoS} computes QoS-weighted betweenness on the Application--Library dependency graph. Three controls registered in Amendment~7 separate substrate from weighting: unweighted betweenness on the same graph scores $0.591$; constant topic weights score $0.595$; permuting QoS profiles scores $0.559$ ($-0.006$, $p = 0.73$). On $I^*$, the gain comes from the Application--Library graph structure, not from QoS contract content.

\noindent\textbf{The first-order expansion of the oracle.} The strongest reference is a post hoc closed-form first-order expansion of $I^*$:
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:analytic_istar}
\hat{I}^*_1(v) = \sum_{t \in \text{pub}(v)} \frac{|\text{sub}(t)|}{|\text{pub}(t)|},
\end{equation}
where $|\text{pub}(t)| \ge 1$ holds for all published topics $t \in \text{pub}(v)$ by construction ($v$ publishes to $t$), preventing any division by zero. It reaches mean $\rho = 0.808$ $[0.755, 0.853]$ ($\rho_{>0} = 0.631$): direct subscriber loss is most of what $I^*$ measures, and \texttt{InDeg} is a coarser version of the same quantity. As a reference it bounds the mean rather than every fold: on four folds (AV, Financial Trading, Healthcare, Industrial SCADA) \texttt{InDeg} slightly exceeds it (by $0.005$--$0.038$).

\noindent\textbf{Beyond the reachability oracle.} Table~\ref{tab:independent_oracles} scores the rankers against the two other oracles. On full-population $I_{\text{dyn}}$, which agrees with $I^*$ at $\rho = 0.711$ $[0.625, 0.794]$, the references stay ahead of every GNN (first-order expansion $0.706$, \texttt{InDeg} $0.664$, against $0.496$--$0.615$), and \texttt{Topo-QoS} reaches $0.471$. Because $I_{\text{dyn}}$ shares the topology and the first-order effect with $I^*$, the partial correlation shows how much of each ranking survives outside the oracle it was fitted to or restates. After the rank of $I^*$ is removed, \texttt{InDeg} keeps $0.272$ $[0.188, 0.366]$, \texttt{Reach} $0.117$, \texttt{Topo-QoS} $0.134$ and the GNNs $0.107$--$0.173$, every interval excluding zero. After the first-order expansion is removed instead, the order reverses: \texttt{InDeg} keeps $0.079$, and the raw-multigraph GNNs keep most ($0.212$--$0.217$), so the learners carry queue-flow signal beyond the direct count that the count does not. Learning from the right labels matters more than the graph model. A gradient-boosted model over the counts, the first-order expansion and QoS features, trained on $I_{\text{dyn}}$ labels of the training scenarios, ranks held-out architectures at $0.799$, above the first-order expansion ($+0.094$, Holm $p = 0.019$) and \texttt{InDeg} ($+0.135$, Holm $p = 0.019$; Amendment~11, Family B); its QoS features carry that gain ($+0.095$ over the same model without them, Holm $p = 0.014$), whereas on $I^*$ they add nothing ($-0.003$, $p = 0.62$; Family C). The GNN given the same labels does not: \texttt{GAT-P-QoS}$\to$dyn reaches $0.598$, below the first-order expansion ($-0.108$, Holm $p = 0.014$) and the gradient-boosted surrogate ($-0.201$, Holm $p = 0.0015$), and no better than the same GNN trained on $I^*$ ($-0.008$; Amendment~14, F5). These surrogates are trained on the oracle they are scored on; their value is cost, since labelling $I_{\text{dyn}}$ took $12.7$ CPU-hours for the corpus (Section~\ref{sec:rq4}). Trained on $I^*$ instead, the same gradient-boosted model is less robust across the three oracles than the first-order expansion alone (worst-case $\rho$ $-0.121$, Holm $p = 0.001$; Family A).

\noindent\textbf{The multi-criteria oracle favours different rankers.} On $I_{\text{comp}}$, which adds fragmentation, throughput and flow disruption (Section~\ref{sec:4.3}), QoS-weighted centrality ranks above the direct-dependent reference ($\rho = 0.702$ $[0.649, 0.753]$ against $0.650$; the reference ahead on only 3 of 12 folds, $p = 0.110$), and raw total degree, the weakest ranker on $I^*$, reaches $0.719$ (the reference ahead on 4 of 12 folds, Holm $p = 0.220$), while \texttt{Reach} falls to $0.302$. Both leaders are proximate to $I_{\text{comp}}$'s fragmentation and flow terms (Section~\ref{sec:4.4}), so their lead is read as the same kind of overlap as the references' lead on $I^*$, not as predictive skill. The learned engines without a centrality prior collapse on this oracle ($0.144$--$0.334$; the sum-aggregation GNNs of Section~\ref{sec:rq2} reach $0.41$--$0.44$); the two hybrids, which carry \texttt{Topo-QoS} as a prior, keep $0.58$. No ranker is best on all three oracles.

\noindent\textbf{Identifying the critical set.} Rank correlation over all Applications is not the operational question in a CI gate, which is whether the true top of the ranking gets flagged. Figure~\ref{fig:recall} gives, for each ranker, the share of the true top-$20\%$ set on $I^*$ that falls within its top $k\%$, with ties resolved in expectation (Amendment~12). At $k = 20\%$, \texttt{GAT-P-QoS} recovers $0.48$ of the critical set and \texttt{Topo-QoS} $0.38$, consistent with their Overlap@$K$ ($0.454$ and $0.388$): about half of what a top-$20\%$ gate flags is not in the true top $20\%$, and about half of the true top $20\%$ is missed. Recovering $80\%$ of the critical set requires flagging the top $40\%$ by \texttt{GAT-P-QoS} ($0.81$), and \texttt{Topo-QoS} does not reach $80\%$ within $50\%$. The references do little better: at $k = 20\%$, \texttt{InDeg} recovers $0.49$ (tie-breaking bounds $0.48$--$0.50$), and $80\%$ needs the top $45\%$ by \texttt{InDeg} ($0.83$) or by the first-order expansion ($0.89$); only the first-order expansion reaches $90\%$ within the top $50\%$, and \texttt{Reach} does not reach $80\%$ within $50\%$. On the earlier $n = 30$ $I_{\text{dyn}}$ sample (the right panel of Figure~\ref{fig:recall}; not recomputed on the full population), \texttt{GAT-P-QoS} reaches $0.65$ at $40\%$ and $0.80$ at $50\%$, against $0.83$ at $40\%$ for the \texttt{InDeg} reference.

\noindent\textbf{The ``QoS'' factor is confounded with the reference count.} Its main effect ($+0.073$) is not significant after Holm correction ($p_{\text{Holm}} = 0.127$), and it could not be attributed to QoS content even if it were. In the node features, $w_{\text{in}}$ is the sum of incoming dependency weights, a QoS-weighted version of \texttt{InDeg}; the QoS-off arms zero it, and so remove a version of the quantity closest to $I^*$'s first wave along with the QoS contracts. A rerun of the $2\times2$ with $w_{\text{in}}$ kept in both arms was not performed. The closed-form controls point the same way: unweighted betweenness ($0.591$) and constant topic weights ($0.595$) match or exceed QoS-weighted betweenness ($0.553$), and permuting QoS profiles across topics changes nothing ($\Delta = -0.006$ $[-0.025, +0.015]$, $p = 0.733$). On $I^*$ we find no evidence that QoS contract content carries ranking signal.

\noindent\textbf{Relation typing under matched capacity.} \texttt{HGT-QoS} remains within $0.013$ of \texttt{GAT-QoS} (Table~\ref{tab:contrasts_matched}). On the raw multigraph this says little about typing, because no message reaches an Application there: a gradient-boosted model over the same per-node features without any graph (\texttt{GBM-Feat}, Amendment~8) reaches $0.642$, level with \texttt{GAT-QoS}, and removing HGT's reverse pass, the only route by which messages reach Applications, changes nothing (\texttt{HGT-QoS-U}, $-0.010$, $p = 0.91$; Supplementary Section~\ref{S-supp:amendments}). On the dependency graph, where messages do arrive, \texttt{HGT-P-QoS} reaches $0.514$ with a mean within-fold seed SD of $0.254$, against $0.748$ and $0.030$ for \texttt{GAT-P-QoS}. This is one untuned configuration (width 100, no hyperparameter search), and averaging its five seeds' predictions raises it to $0.618$; we therefore read it as seed instability of that configuration, not as evidence against relational typing in general.
