\subsection{RQ1: SaG's Engines Against Structural Baselines}
\label{sec:rq1}
\label{sec:rq1-hybrid}
\label{sec:rq1-loso}

\paragraph{Summary} \emph{On SaG's derived dependency graph, counting a component's dependents ranks simulated cascade impact as well as any engine in the study: \texttt{InDeg} reaches $\rho = 0.764$ ($\rho_{>0} = 0.516$ on active components) and beats closed-form centrality (\texttt{Topo-QoS}, $0.553$) on all twelve held-out architectures ($+0.211$, Holm $p = 0.002$). For Applications, \texttt{InDeg} is mathematically identical to counting distinct subscribers across published topics (publish--subscribe afferent coupling / AIS). A graph neural network that reads the dependency graph reaches the same level (\texttt{GAT-P-QoS}, $\rho = 0.748$), while an analytic first-order approximation of the simulator achieves $\rho = 0.808$ ($\rho_{>0} = 0.631$). Both hybrid engines significantly outperform closed-form centrality under their registered rules ($+0.103$ and $+0.130$, Holm $p \le 0.0068$).}

Each of the twelve folds holds out one scenario and trains on the remaining eleven, scored on the Application node set (26 to 300 nodes, $K$ between 5 and 60). \noindent\textbf{Counting dependents formalizes afferent coupling.} \texttt{InDeg}, the number of direct dependents of $v$ on the derived graph, reaches $\rho = 0.764$ ($\rho_{>0} = 0.516$) and beats \texttt{Topo-QoS} on every fold; \texttt{Reach}, the number of transitive dependents, reaches $0.732$ (11/12). For an Application, \texttt{InDeg} is identically the number of distinct subscribers across all topics it publishes: publish--subscribe afferent coupling \cite{martin2003agile,rud2006ais,bogner2017maintainability}. Deriving topic-mediated dependencies is what makes this metric computable from architecture manifests without running code. Furthermore, transitive reachability profits from the derived library rule: without Rule~5, \texttt{Reach} falls by $0.058$ (9/12 folds, Holm $p = 0.0068$; Amendment~10).

\noindent\textbf{Learners on the dependency graph match the count.} The same graph neural networks, trained on the dependency graph instead of the raw multigraph, reach $\rho = 0.748$ (\texttt{GAT-P-QoS}), level with \texttt{InDeg} ($-0.017$, Holm $p = 1.000$). Anchoring the learner on the \texttt{InDeg} score yields $\rho = 0.758$ (Hybrid-GAT-P), level with both. On the active stratum, \texttt{Hybrid-GAT-P} achieves the highest correlation ($\rho_{>0} = 0.537$, above \texttt{InDeg}'s $0.516$), though this margin is within statistical noise. In contrast, heterogeneous transformers (\texttt{HGT-P-QoS}) fail to train stably on the projection (mean $\rho = 0.514$, seed spread $0.208$), showing that relational parameterization impairs convergence on sparse dependency graphs.

\noindent\textbf{Hybrids outperform closed-form centrality.} Hybrid-HGT reaches $\rho = 0.657$ ($+0.103$, Holm $p = 0.0068$) and Hybrid-GAT reaches $\rho = 0.683$ ($+0.130$, Holm $p = 0.0029$), each on 11 of 12 folds. Both survive omnibus Holm correction across all thirteen confirmatory contrasts ($p_{\text{omni}} = 0.041$ and $0.019$). However, on their own, the raw-multigraph learned engines are statistically on par with \texttt{Topo-QoS} (\texttt{HGT-QoS} $+0.069$, $p = 0.266$; \texttt{GAT-QoS} $+0.082$, $p = 0.233$).

\noindent\textbf{Where the closed-form gain comes from.} \texttt{Topo} reads betweenness from the application-layer graph, whereas \texttt{Topo-QoS} computes QoS-weighted betweenness on the Application--Library dependency graph. Three controls registered in Amendment~7 separate substrate from weighting: unweighted betweenness on the same graph scores $0.591$; constant topic weights score $0.595$; permuting QoS profiles scores $0.559$ ($-0.006$, $p = 0.73$). The gain is therefore driven by the Application--Library graph structure, not QoS contract content.

\noindent\textbf{The analytic reference ceiling.} To quantify how much of \texttt{InDeg}'s accuracy is driven by the simulator's construction, Table~\ref{tab:hybrid} includes the post hoc closed-form analytic first-order approximation:
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:analytic_istar}
\hat{I}^*_1(v) = \sum_{t \in \text{pub}(v)} \frac{|\text{sub}(t)|}{|\text{pub}(t)|}.
\end{equation}
This first-order expression achieves mean $\rho = 0.808$ $[0.755, 0.853]$ ($\rho_{>0} = 0.631$), demonstrating that direct topological subscriber loss forms the core mechanism of the reachability oracle. However, as a ceiling, it bounds the mean rather than every fold: on four folds (AV, Financial Trading, Healthcare, Industrial SCADA), \texttt{InDeg} slightly exceeds the analytic approximation (by $0.005\text{--}0.038$), while on the remaining eight folds the analytic expression leads.

\noindent\textbf{Validation against independent oracles.} Table~\ref{tab:independent_oracles} reports ranking agreement when the same training-free rankers are evaluated across independent simulation paradigms: the dynamic queue-flow oracle $I_{\text{dyn}}$ and the multi-criteria composite oracle $I_{\text{comp}}$. On $I_{\text{dyn}}$ ($n = 30$ candidates per fold), \texttt{InDeg} achieves $\rho = 0.610$ $[0.475, 0.727]$ ($\rho_{>0} = 0.589$), significantly outperforming closed-form centrality (\texttt{Topo-QoS}, $\rho = 0.393$; $\Delta = +0.217$, 11/12 fold wins, Wilcoxon $p = 0.0034$), while the analytic approximation reaches $\rho = 0.636$ ($\Delta = +0.243$, 11/12, $p = 0.0015$). Because $I_{\text{dyn}}$ measures continuous message delivery degradation under stochastic queueing load rather than graph reachability, this confirms that \texttt{InDeg}'s predictive utility is not an artifact of reachability cascade simulation. On genuine $I_{\text{comp}}$ (exhaustive multi-criteria failure simulation across all 1{,}321 applications), \texttt{Topo-QoS} achieves $\rho = 0.702$ $[0.649, 0.753]$, aligning with its QoS-weighted betweenness paths, while \texttt{InDeg} achieves $\rho = 0.650$ ($-0.052$, $p = 0.110$) and \texttt{Analytic $I^*$} achieves $\rho = 0.636$ ($-0.066$, $p = 0.064$). Conversely, \texttt{Reach} falls to $0.504$ on $I_{\text{dyn}}$ and $0.302$ on $I_{\text{comp}}$ ($-0.400$, $p = 0.0005$), indicating that transitive reachability is specialized to topological cascade reachability.

\label{sec:rq2}

When capacity is matched, relation typing adds nothing (typing main effect $-0.014$). \noindent\textbf{QoS feature attribution and confounding.} Table~\ref{tab:contrasts_matched} shows that the QoS main effect ($+0.073$) is not statistically significant after Holm correction ($p_{\text{Holm}} = 0.127$). In the node feature encoding, $w_{\text{in}}$ (\texttt{qos\_weight\_in}) is the sum of incoming edge weights on the analysis graph---a weighted in-degree. The QoS ablation zeroed this column, thereby removing the dependent count itself. As demonstrated by the registered closed-form controls in Amendment~7 (\path{results/qos_attribution_controls.json}), unweighted betweenness ($0.591$) and constant topic weights ($0.595$) match or exceed QoS-weighted betweenness ($0.553$), and permuting QoS profiles across topics yields no significant difference ($\Delta = -0.006$ $[-0.025, +0.015]$, $p = 0.733$), confirming that the observed gain is driven by dependency graph structure rather than QoS contract parameters.

\noindent\textbf{Relation typing under matched capacity.} \texttt{HGT-QoS} remains within $0.013$ of \texttt{GAT-QoS} (Table~\ref{tab:contrasts_matched}). In the single untuned configuration evaluated (width 100, default hyperparameters), \texttt{HGT-P-QoS} failed to train stably on the dependency projection (mean $\rho = 0.514$, seed spread $0.208$), whereas \texttt{GAT-P-QoS} converged smoothly ($\rho = 0.748$; Table~\ref{tab:hybrid}). Relational typing remains untested in tuned hyperparameter configurations or architectures where message passing reaches scored nodes.
