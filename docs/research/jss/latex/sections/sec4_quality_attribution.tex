\section{Interpretable Attribution as a Quality Baseline}
\label{sec:4}

To provide actionable explanations alongside predictive rankings, SaG decomposes component criticality into a multi-dimensional, standards-grounded quality attribution profile.

\subsection{Grounding in ISO/IEC Standards}
\label{sec:4.1}

Following \textbf{ISO/IEC 25010:2023} (Product Quality) \cite{iso25010_2023} and \textbf{ISO/IEC 25019:2023} (Quality-in-Use) \cite{iso25019_2023}, we define two core formal constructs:
\begin{itemize}
    \item \textbf{Component Criticality ($D_1$):} The degree to which the failure or degradation of a component reduces the system's capacity to deliver required services within its operational context.
    \item \textbf{Relationship Criticality ($D_2$):} The degree of service degradation resulting from the severance of a specific dependency channel while both endpoint components remain operational.
\end{itemize}

Criticality is evaluated primarily across two orthogonal quality dimensions: \textbf{Reliability ($R$)} and \textbf{Maintainability ($M$)}.

\noindent\textit{Coverage Scope:} SaG focuses on Reliability and Maintainability. Safety (which requires hazard catalogues) and Security (which requires explicit threat models) are declared external to purely structural analysis and require domain-specific hazard analysis (e.g., ISO 26262 ASIL).

\subsection{Composite Quality Score Formulation}
\label{sec:4.2}

All raw structural and code metrics are rank-normalized to $[0, 1]$. The quality sub-characteristics are formulated as follows:

\begin{enumerate}
    \item \textbf{Fault Tolerance ($FT(v)$):} Measures error propagation potential on the transpose graph $G_{\text{analysis}}^\top$ (where edges follow failure propagation from dependency to dependent):
    \begin{equation}
    FT(v) = w_{\text{rpr}} \cdot \text{RPR}(v) + w_{\text{indeg}} \cdot \text{Deg}_{\text{in}}(v) + w_{\text{depth}} \cdot \text{DepthPotential}(v)
    \end{equation}
    \item \textbf{Availability ($A(v)$):} Identifies structural single points of failure:
    \begin{equation}
    A(v) = w_{\text{art}} \cdot \text{ArticulationScore}(v) + w_{\text{bridge}} \cdot \text{BridgeCentrality}(v)
    \end{equation}
    \item \textbf{Reliability ($R(v)$):} Blends Fault Tolerance and Availability hierarchically:
    \begin{equation}
    R(v) = \alpha \cdot FT(v) + (1 - \alpha) \cdot A(v), \quad \alpha = 0.36
    \end{equation}
    The intra-dimension weights are audited for AHP consistency ($CR \le 0.08$).
    \item \textbf{Maintainability ($M(v)$):} Evaluates structural coupling combined with code-level static analysis:
    \begin{equation}
    M(v) = 0.50 \cdot \text{Betweenness}(v) + 0.35 \cdot \text{Deg}_{\text{out}}(v) + 0.15 \cdot \text{CodeQualityPenalty}(v)
    \end{equation}
    where $\text{CodeQualityPenalty}(v)$ is derived from SonarQube metrics (complexity, LCOM, coupling).
\end{enumerate}

The composite quality score $Q(v)$ combines both dimensions:
\begin{equation}
Q(v) = 0.80 \cdot R(v) + 0.20 \cdot M(v)
\end{equation}

Components are categorized into adaptive criticality tiers using box-plot thresholds:
\begin{itemize}
    \item \textbf{CRITICAL:} $Q > Q_3 + 1.5 \cdot \text{IQR}$
    \item \textbf{HIGH:} $Q_3 < Q \le Q_3 + 1.5 \cdot \text{IQR}$
    \item \textbf{MEDIUM:} $Q_1 < Q \le Q_3$
    \item \textbf{MINIMAL:} $Q \le Q_1$
\end{itemize}

This enables targeted diagnostics: a service scoring high on $A$ but low on $FT$ is diagnosed as a pure SPOF requiring replication, whereas a service scoring high on $FT$ is an error cascade hub requiring circuit breakers.
