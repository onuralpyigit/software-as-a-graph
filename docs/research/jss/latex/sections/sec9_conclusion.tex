\section{Conclusion}
\label{sec:9}

This study showed that typed, QoS-aware architecture models support accurate pre-deployment ranking of components by cascading-failure impact, without runtime telemetry. Software-as-a-Graph turns architecture descriptions into typed multigraphs with QoS-weighted dependencies. Engines built on this representation were evaluated under leave-one-scenario-out cross-validation over twelve synthetic architectures and zero-shot on five independently authored models of open-source systems.

The representation carries much of the signal: QoS-aware projection raises closed-form ranking from $\rho = 0.349$ (unweighted centrality) to $0.553$ on every held-out architecture, and a learned engine on its own is statistically on par with this closed-form engine rather than better than it. For learned engines, a control matched in capacity and edge-channel width showed that SaG's QoS edge encoding is the working ingredient ($+0.073$ on 10 of 12 folds), while relation-specific weights add nothing beyond it. Learned engines transfer well beyond their training distribution ($\rho = 0.760$--$0.805$ against $0.511$--$0.526$ on the system models, with roughly double the top-$K$ critical-set overlap). Learned and closed-form reasoning are complementary. The hybrid engines, in which a learned engine corrects the closed-form score, are the most accurate on architectures resembling the training distribution ($\rho = 0.657$ and $0.683$), while pure learned engines transfer better to substantially different systems ($0.760$ and $0.805$ against the hybrids' $0.695$ and $0.662$). On the synthetic folds the hybrids significantly outperform closed-form ranking on 11 of 12 folds ($+0.103$ and $+0.130$, Holm $p \le 0.0068$), each as registered before its experiment, and both survive a single Holm correction over all eleven registered contrasts ($p = 0.034$ and $0.016$). All of these $p$-values are nominal, because the cross-validation folds share training scenarios. An ISO/IEC 25010 attribution layer complements the rankings by naming the remediation each flagged component calls for.

For practice, SaG offers a training-free closed-form engine for lightweight CI gates, the hybrid engine for the most accurate ranking on familiar architectures, and a QoS-aware learned engine for transfer to substantially different systems. The next steps are to have the open-source system models independently re-modeled and measure inter-modeler agreement, to combine the hybrids' in-distribution accuracy with the pure engines' transfer, to retarget the engines on the behavioral queue-flow oracle, to extract system models directly from deployment manifests, and to validate the rankings against production incident data.
