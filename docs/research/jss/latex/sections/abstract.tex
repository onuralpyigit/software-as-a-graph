Assessing distributed system dependability before deployment is hindered by absent runtime telemetry and static code analysis's blindness to communication topology. We present Software-as-a-Graph (SaG), a static system analysis framework that constructs typed multigraphs from Architecture-as-Code manifests across five entity types. SaG pairs two parameter-independent pathways: a relation-specific Heterogeneous Graph Transformer (HGT) with Quality-of-Service (QoS) edge encodings to forecast cascading failure blast radii, and an interpretable ISO/IEC 25010 layer attributing fragility to Availability (single points of failure) or Fault Tolerance (cascade hubs) to guide targeted repairs.

Across eleven synthetic scenarios and five open-source reference systems: (1) relation typing gives a small but exceptionally consistent gain over homogeneous learning at matched training sets and model selection --- $\Delta\rho = +0.082$ under distribution shift, winning \emph{all twelve} folds ($p = 0.0005$), replicated by the unweighted pair ($+0.064$, 12/12); (2) against a training-free QoS-weighted centrality baseline the learned model establishes no advantage ($+0.058$, $p = 0.204$ at fixed configuration; $+0.115$, $p = 0.052$ under nested selection); (3) on those systems it transfers poorly --- $\rho = 0.125$ against $0.516$ and $0.511$ for two training-free references on identical labels, negative on two of five --- yet its own prediction spread flags every one of them, so the failure is detectable at inference time without labels; (4) an HGT forward pass costs $43.7\,\text{ms}$ at 2{,}000 components, the complete gate $0.02$--$27.4\,\text{s}$. Training a neural model is therefore justified by relational attention, per-relationship criticality, standards-grounded attribution, and this capacity for self-diagnosis --- not by ranking accuracy. Findings (1)--(2) predate a repair to the generator's QoS sampling and await re-measurement.
