Publish--subscribe systems decouple components in space and time, which also hides how failures propagate. We present Software-as-a-Graph (SaG), a pre-deployment analysis framework that converts architecture descriptions into typed multigraphs with Quality-of-Service (QoS) weighted dependencies and ranks components by cascading-failure impact without runtime telemetry. SaG provides a training-free closed-form engine, graph-learning engines with 16-dimensional QoS edge encodings, and hybrid engines in which a learned model corrects the closed-form score, plus an ISO/IEC 25010 layer that explains why a component is critical. Under leave-one-scenario-out cross-validation over twelve synthetic architectures, SaG's QoS-aware projection improves ranking over unweighted centrality on every held-out architecture (Spearman $\rho = 0.553$ vs.\ $0.349$). A capacity- and channel-matched control shows that the QoS edge encoding, not relation-specific weights, drives learned accuracy. On their own, learned engines are statistically on par with the closed-form engine. The hybrid engines are the most accurate ($\rho = 0.657$ and $0.683$) and outperform it on 11 of 12 folds under decision rules registered before their runs. Pure learned engines transfer best to substantially different systems: on hand-authored models of five open-source systems they rank zero-shot at $\rho = 0.760$--$0.805$, against $0.511$--$0.526$ for every training-free score, and they roughly double top-$K$ critical-set overlap. Neural inference takes milliseconds. QoS-aware typed graphs thus give pre-deployment dependability analysis a strong foundation.
