Asynchronous publish--subscribe architectures pose a pre-deployment visibility barrier: bug-free service code can still harbor outages from hidden single points of failure or mismatched middleware contracts. We present Software-as-a-Graph (SaG), a static framework converting Architecture-as-Code manifests into typed multigraphs with an ISO/IEC 25010 attribution layer, and evaluate a Heterogeneous Graph Transformer with Quality-of-Service edge encodings against homogeneous learning and training-free centrality across twelve inductive folds and five open-source systems.

The headline is a boundary rather than a gain: the pre-registered contrast --- learned model against QoS-weighted centrality --- is null ($+0.085$, $p = 0.151$). In a post-hoc $2 \times 2$ ablation, relation typing and the QoS edge channel each carry a main effect ($\Delta\rho = +0.134$ and $+0.187$, Holm-corrected $p = 0.0015$), but interact sub-additively: the difference of differences is $-0.199$, negative on all twelve folds ($p = 0.0005$, CI $[-0.258, -0.147]$), so typing is worth $+0.234$ to a model lacking the QoS channel and $+0.035$ to one that has it. The two are substitutes rather than complements --- both encode which relation an edge crosses --- and the oracle suggests why: its ordering is recovered at mean $\rho = 0.965$ by a relabeling carrying no QoS term. Both large effects are measured against a baseline that itself loses to untrained centrality, so they are recoveries from a deficit rather than absolute gains. Zero-shot transfer to real systems falls from $\rho = 0.767$ to $+0.265$ on components that propagate failures, inverting on two of five. We report these limits as the contribution.
