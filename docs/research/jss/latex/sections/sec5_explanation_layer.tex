\section{The Explanation Layer: Standards-Grounded Criticality Attribution}
\label{sec:5}

The predictive pathway of Section~\ref{sec:4} forecasts \emph{where} systemic risk lies. However, scalar ranking alone does not indicate \emph{how to remediate} vulnerability. A component may be critical because it represents an unreplicated single point of failure, because it propagates error cascades widely, or because it exhibits severe coupling debt. These distinct failure modes necessitate different engineering remediations: replicating a broker, inserting circuit breakers, or refactoring module dependencies.

To provide actionable root-cause diagnoses, SaG pairs its predictor with a deterministic, standards-grounded quality profile. Operating over the typed node features (Section~\ref{sec:3.4}) on $G_{\text{analysis}}$, this explanation layer shares no parameters with the neural model and functions as an independent triage mechanism (Figure~\ref{fig:1}).

\subsection{Grounding in ISO/IEC Standards}
\label{sec:5.1}

In accordance with \textbf{ISO/IEC 25010:2023} (Product Quality Model) \cite{iso25010_2023}, \textbf{ISO/IEC 25019:2023} (Quality-in-Use) \cite{iso25019_2023}, and \textbf{ISO/IEC 25022:2016} (Measurement of Quality-in-Use) \cite{iso25022_2016}, SaG formalizes two primary criticality constructs:
\begin{itemize}
    \item \textbf{Component Criticality ($D_1$):} The degree to which the failure or severe degradation of an individual component reduces the system's ability to deliver required functionality within its operational context of use.
    \item \textbf{Relationship Criticality ($D_2$):} The degree of systemic service degradation resulting from the severance or failure of a specific communication channel while both endpoint components remain operational.
\end{itemize}

Criticality is evaluated across two orthogonal quality characteristics: \textbf{Reliability ($R$)} and \textbf{Maintainability ($M$)}. The two dimensions are separated because they imply distinct architectural remedies. Table~\ref{tab:3} outlines this Reliability--Maintainability (RM) quality decomposition, mapping ISO/IEC sub-characteristics to architectural diagnostics, underlying graph metrics, and targeted remediations.

\noindent\textit{Coverage Scope:} SaG focuses specifically on Reliability and Maintainability. Safety (which requires domain-specific hazard logs, such as ISO 26262 ASIL ratings) and Security (which requires threat models, such as STRIDE) fall outside structural topology analysis and represent domain-specific extensions.

\subsection{Composite Quality Score Formulation}
\label{sec:5.2}

All raw topological and code metrics are rank-normalized to $[0, 1]$. Quality sub-characteristics are formulated hierarchically using the Analytic Hierarchy Process (AHP) \cite{saaty1980ahp}:
\begin{enumerate}
    \item \textbf{Fault Tolerance ($FT(v)$):} Measures error cascade potential on the transpose graph $G_{\text{analysis}}^\top$ (where edges follow failure propagation from dependency to dependent):
    \begin{equation}
    FT(v) = 0.45 \cdot \text{RPR}(v) + 0.30 \cdot \text{Deg}_{\text{in}}(v) + 0.25 \cdot \text{CDPot}_{\text{enh}}(v)
    \end{equation}
    where $\text{RPR}(v)$ is Reverse PageRank, $\text{Deg}_{\text{in}}(v)$ is normalized in-degree, and $\text{CDPot}_{\text{enh}}(v)$ is the enhanced Cascade Depth Potential.
    \item \textbf{Availability ($A(v)$):} Identifies structural single points of failure (SPOFs) across five terms:
    \begin{equation}
    \label{eq:availability}
    A(v) = 0.2563 \cdot \text{AP}_c^{\text{dir}}(v) + 0.1998 \cdot \text{QSPOF}(v) + 0.1998 \cdot \text{BR}(v) + 0.2563 \cdot \text{CDI}(v) + 0.0878 \cdot w_V(v)
    \end{equation}
    where $\text{AP}_c^{\text{dir}}(v)$ is Directed Articulation Point severity, $\text{QSPOF}(v)$ is QoS-weighted Single Point of Failure severity, $\text{BR}(v)$ is Bridge Ratio (edge-level irrecoverability), $\text{CDI}(v)$ is Connectivity Degradation Index, and $w_V(v)$ is the component's intrinsic weight.
    \item \textbf{Reliability ($R(v)$):} Blends Fault Tolerance and Availability:
    \begin{equation}
    R(v) = r_\alpha \cdot FT(v) + (1 - r_\alpha) \cdot A(v), \quad r_\alpha = 0.36
    \end{equation}
    The blend weight $r_\alpha = 0.36$ is screened in Supplementary Section~S1. The intra-dimension weights apply $\lambda = 0.70$ shrinkage blending with a uniform prior (Section~\ref{sec:7.3}). Because three of the comparison matrices are rank-one by construction (implying near-zero $CR$ by design; Supplementary Section~S4), these weights represent a structured multi-criteria baseline convention designed for qualitative attribution rather than scalar ranking optimization.
    \item \textbf{Maintainability ($M(v)$):} Evaluates structural coupling combined with code-level static analysis:
    \begin{equation}
    M(v) = 0.35 \cdot \text{BT}(v) + 0.30 \cdot w_{\text{out}}(v) + 0.15 \cdot \text{CQP}(v) + 0.12 \cdot \text{CouplingRisk}_{\text{enh}}(v) + 0.08 \cdot (1 - \text{CC}(v))
    \end{equation}
    where $\text{BT}(v)$ is Betweenness Centrality, $w_{\text{out}}(v)$ is QoS-weighted efferent coupling, $\text{CQP}(v)$ is Code Quality Penalty, $\text{CouplingRisk}_{\text{enh}}(v)$ is coupling imbalance, and $\text{CC}(v)$ is local Clustering Coefficient.
\end{enumerate}

The baseline composite quality score $Q(v)$ combines both dimensions:
\begin{equation}
Q(v) = 0.80 \cdot R(v) + 0.20 \cdot M(v)
\end{equation}

Components are categorized into adaptive criticality tiers using Tukey quartile thresholds: \textbf{CRITICAL} ($Q > Q_3 + 1.5 \cdot \text{IQR}$), \textbf{HIGH} ($Q_3 < Q \le Q_3 + 1.5 \cdot \text{IQR}$), \textbf{MEDIUM} ($Q_1 < Q \le Q_3$), and \textbf{MINIMAL} ($Q \le Q_1$). This enables targeted remediation: high $A$ with low $FT$ indicates an isolated SPOF calling for broker or host replication, whereas high $FT$ denotes an error-cascade hub requiring circuit breakers, rate limiting, and bulkhead isolation.

\subsection{Prescriptive Remediation and Counterfactual Verification}
\label{sec:5.3}

Downstream in the pipeline, once the explanation layer attributes an architectural root cause, automated refactoring operators generate candidate repair manifests (e.g., replicating an SPOF broker, inserting circuit breakers, or decoupling publisher--subscriber feeds). 

To verify proposed interventions prior to deployment without risking regressions, SaG incorporates a closed-loop \textbf{Prescribe Stage} powered by the \texttt{EditVerifier}:
\begin{itemize}
    \item \textbf{Counterfactual Mutation Verification:} The verifier constructs an in-memory mutated graph $G'$ reflecting the candidate refactoring and evaluates systemic risk. To bypass expensive all-pairs CDI recomputation ($\mathcal{O}(|V|^2 + |V||E|)$), candidate screening executes via localized HGT-QoS forward inference ($<56\,\text{ms}$), backed by multi-threshold cascade simulation. An edit is accepted if it strictly reduces systemic impact beyond simulator seed variance ($\Delta \bar{I}_{\text{comp}} > \kappa \cdot \sigma_{\text{seed}}$, $\kappa \ge 1.0$) across all thresholds without introducing new articulation points.
    \item \textbf{Relationship-Level Interventions:} For edge-level refactorings (e.g., splitting a congested topic or severing an unstable dependency), the edge removal oracle ($I_{\text{edge}}(u,v)$, Eq.~\eqref{eq:edge_crit}) verifies whether candidate edge modifications mitigate systemic vulnerability.
\end{itemize}
