\section{A Proposed Explanation Layer (Not Evaluated)}
\label{sec:5}

A ranking says where risk is highest, not how to reduce it. A component may be critical because it is an unreplicated single point of failure, an error-propagating cascade hub, or a high-coupling maintainability bottleneck, and each calls for a different intervention: replication, circuit breakers, or decoupling. SaG includes a layer that attributes a flagged component to one of these causes. \textbf{This layer is a design proposal. Its attributions have not been validated against practitioner judgment or against the outcome of applying the remediation it names, and nothing in Sections~\ref{sec:6}--\ref{sec:8} evaluates it.} It is described here because it is part of the released tool and because its inputs overlap the rankers' features; it is not one of this paper's contributions, and it is not used as a ranker (as a ranker it is weak: its composite reaches $\rho = 0.200$ against $I^*$ at the shipped setting; Supplementary Section~\ref{S-supp:params}).

\subsection{Structure}
\label{sec:5.1}
\label{sec:5.2}

Following ISO/IEC 25010:2023 \cite{iso25010_2023} and ISO/IEC 25019:2023 \cite{iso25019_2023}, the layer profiles each component along \textbf{Fault Tolerance ($FT$)}, which informs circuit breakers and redundancy; \textbf{Availability ($A$)}, which informs replication; and \textbf{Maintainability ($M$)}, which informs decoupling and refactoring. Each is a weighted sum of rank-normalized graph metrics read from the same node properties as the rankers (Section~\ref{sec:3.5}): Reverse PageRank, in-degree and cascade depth for $FT$; articulation severity, QoS-weighted single-point-of-failure severity, bridge ratio and the Connectivity Degradation Index for $A$; betweenness, efferent coupling, code-quality penalty and clustering for $M$. The composite $Q(v) = 0.80\,R(v) + 0.20\,M(v)$, with $R = 0.36\,FT + 0.64\,A$, flags components above the Tukey upper fence of $Q$ (mean $4.2\%$ of components), and the $FT$/$A$/$M$ profile names the remediation class (Figure~\ref{fig:rm}; example card: Supplementary Section~\ref{S-supp:card}). The full formulas, the AHP weights and their consistency diagnostics are in Supplementary Sections~\ref{S-supp:rm-formulas} and~\ref{S-supp:ahp}. Three of those weight vectors encode a declared priority vector rather than independent elicitation, and moving the intra-dimension weights toward the elicited judgment lowers the layer's rank correlation ($0.319 \to 0.200$; Supplementary Section~\ref{S-supp:params}). Validating the attributions is the layer's principal open question (Section~\ref{sec:limitations}).
