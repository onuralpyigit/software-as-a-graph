# Referee Report — JSS Special Issue on AI Techniques for Performance, Reliability, and Sustainability of Modern Software Systems

**Manuscript:** *Software-as-a-Graph: Heterogeneous Graph Learning for Pre-Deployment Dependability Analysis of Asynchronous and Event-Driven Distributed Systems* (Yigit, Buzluca)
**Source reviewed:** `docs/research/jss/manuscript.md` (full text, §1–§9, declarations, references), `latex/highlights.tex`, `latex/LENGTH_JUSTIFICATION.md`, checked against the JSS Guide for Authors. Three claims were spot-checked against the replication code (noted where relevant).

---

## 1. Summary

The paper proposes Software-as-a-Graph (SaG). SaG turns publish–subscribe architecture manifests into a typed multigraph and a QoS-weighted `DEPENDS_ON` projection. A Heterogeneous Graph Transformer with 16-D QoS edge encodings (`HGT-QoS`) is then trained to predict a simulated cascade-reachability label, $I^*(v)$, and the paper adds an ISO/IEC 25010/25019 attribution layer (RM) for diagnosis. The method is evaluated with 12-fold leave-one-scenario-out cross-validation over synthetic topologies from one generator, with zero-shot transfer to five hand-authored models of open-source systems, and with a cost comparison against the simulator. The main reported contributions are these:
- a negative confirmatory result: `HGT-QoS` does not significantly beat a closed-form QoS-weighted centrality (+0.085, p = 0.151);
- a "substitution" interaction between relation typing and QoS encoding (−0.199, 0/12 folds positive);
- a finding that cold static analysis costs 2–18× the simulation it was meant to replace.

## 2. Overall Impression & Assessment

**Originality — moderate to low.** Each component already exists: HGT [72] comes off the shelf through PyG, the dependency projection extends the authors' RASSE 2025 paper [28], and AHP/ISO-25010 scoring is standard. What is new is applying them to pub-sub dependability, plus the factorial ablation. The paper's own results undercut the novelty of the headline method. The learned model ties a two-term closed-form baseline, and no published learned-criticality method (DrBC, FINDER) is reproduced. The authors concede this in §8.4.

**Significance — limited in the current form.** The central use case is predicting failure impact before deployment without simulation. The paper's own evidence undermines it on three counts:
- The label is a BFS reachability computation that is cheaper to compute exactly (0.08–4.5 s) than the features used to predict it (0.16–79.3 s).
- The claimed advantages over simulation — scoring hosts, and robustness to missing rates and payloads — are never evaluated.
- Nothing is validated against a real failure.

**Methodological soundness — mixed.** The statistical reporting is unusually candid and careful: fold-level Wilcoxon tests, bootstrap CIs, a Fisher-z check of the interaction, an active-stratum re-analysis, a label-noise ceiling, and a registered analysis plan. The experimental design, however, leaves the headline 2×2 factorial confounded with edge-channel width and capacity (Major 1). Several claims in the Abstract, Introduction and Conclusion also go beyond, or contradict, what §7 reports (Major 3).

**Suitability for a Q1 venue / this special issue.** The topic fits the special issue well: AI for reliability, plus a sustainability and cost angle. JSS explicitly welcomes negative results, and the honest reporting is a real asset. The manuscript still reads like an accumulated revision log rather than a paper. Its main scientific claim (substitution) rests on a confounded contrast, and its framing still sells a method ("HGT-QoS accurately tracked…", "Dual-Engine … gives reliable triage") that the results do not support. It is not publishable as is. It could become a solid negative-results / empirical-boundary paper after substantial rework.

## 3. Major Comments

### M1. The 2×2 factorial does not hold the edge channel constant across its rows, and "QoS-absent" arms still see QoS through their node features.
> **Correction (2026-09-23, after re-checking the harness).** An earlier version of this comment said the QoS-masked `HGT` still receives the scalar QoS weight $w(e)$ as edge feature 0. That is wrong for the evaluated runs. Every LOSO fit goes through `_mask_qos_in_graph` (`reproduce/main_table.py:866-897`), which sets every edge `weight` to 1.0 before tensorisation. `HGT` therefore sees a constant weight, a constant path count and the relation one-hot, and has no scalar QoS channel. The comment below replaces the original.

Two asymmetries remain in the design.

**(a) The Q factor is not the same treatment in both rows.**
- **Untyped row:** Q adds a 1-dimensional scalar $w(e)$ (GAT-N → GAT-N-QoS).
- **Typed row:** Q adds the full 16-dimensional encoding (HGT → HGT-QoS).

The interaction is therefore confounded with edge-channel width, in addition to the 15.4× capacity gap already disclosed. The registered `GAT-N-QoS16-C` arm (untyped, capacity-matched, 16-D channel) together with `GAT-N-C` would form a 2×2 matched on both. It is implemented but has not been run.

**(b) "QoS-absent" is not QoS-free.** PageRank, reverse PageRank, betweenness and eigenvector centrality — node features 0, 1, 2 and 4 — are computed on the QoS-*weighted* `DEPENDS_ON` projection (`saag/analysis/structural_analyzer.py:328-334`). Every QoS-off arm therefore still receives QoS through its node features. This affects both rows equally, so it does not by itself create the interaction. It does mean the Q factor is "explicit QoS edge channel", not "QoS information", and the text should say so.

**Required:** run the matched 2×2, or at minimum state (a) and (b) wherever the substitution claim is made. Correct Table 5's edge-feature column for `HGT`, which should read "constant weight + relation one-hot".

### M2. The floor arm (GAT-N) is unstable and very likely under-trained, which inflates both main effects and the interaction.
GAT-N's median within-fold seed σ is 0.298, against 0.024–0.114 for every other learned arm (Table 6). §7.3.1 itself observes that "either one [channel] fixes it". That pattern is equally consistent with a different mechanism: *any* added edge signal cures an optimization pathology in an untuned 28k-parameter GAT. The "redundant relation-identity" story is not established by this data. Hyperparameters were deliberately left untuned (§4.1), so the comparison is between arms at unknown distances from their optima. The reported effects are therefore partly effects of the optimizer.

**Required:** run a small, fold-internal, equal-budget hyperparameter search per arm (inner-validation only), or at least report learning curves and seed distributions for GAT-N. Also report the 2×2 with GAT-N's failed seeds diagnosed. The four registered control arms (capacity-matched, unidirectional) are "implemented but not run". They should be run before submission rather than listed as future work, because §7.2's entire claim depends on them.

### M3. The Abstract, Introduction, Table 14 and Conclusion overstate or contradict §7.
- **Abstract:** "`HGT-QoS` outperformed untyped networks … (Δρ = +0.234, reaching ρ = 0.638)." The +0.234 is **HGT vs GAT-N** (no QoS), not HGT-QoS. HGT-QoS vs GAT-N-QoS is +0.035, p = 0.129. The same conflation appears in contribution 1 and in Table 14 ("`HGT-QoS` … Δρ = +0.234").
- **Abstract:** "`HGT-QoS` accurately tracked how failures spread in asynchronous event meshes." §7.4 says active-stratum transfer is *unresolved*: CI [−0.053, +0.525] spans zero, and the 3–2 split is "a pattern worth naming, not a tested effect".
- **Conclusion (1):** "strong zero-shot transfer on active failure spreaders (+0.517 Autoware, +0.702 Home Assistant) where training-free baselines fail." This cherry-picks two of five systems and contradicts Table 10.
- **§1.3 boundary condition 2:** The paradigm-alignment claim is stated as "establishing that graph directional biases must correspond to architectural communication semantics". It rests on n = 3 vs n = 2 with overlapping CIs, and it was formulated after the results were seen (see M5).
- **Abstract:** "Using both models together gives reliable critical-set triage." The Dual-Engine Consensus Protocol is **never evaluated**: no precision, coverage, or agreement rate is reported for the top-K intersection. It is a named deliverable with zero evidence and should either be evaluated or be demoted to a suggestion in the Discussion.
- **Abstract / §1.3 / §8.2:** "Allows for sub-second gating when combined with incremental topology caching." §7.5.1 states caching is "not yet implemented". Claims about unbuilt systems do not belong in an abstract.
- **Title:** It advertises "Heterogeneous Graph Learning for … Dependability Analysis", while the confirmatory result is that heterogeneous learning does not beat a closed-form score. The title should reflect the actual finding.

### M4. The value proposition relative to direct simulation is not supported by the paper's own evidence.
§1.3 gives three reasons to learn rather than simulate. None is tested:
1. **"Scoring unsimulated infrastructure entities."** Every learned result is scored on $V_{\text{app}}$ only. Host scoring is never evaluated. §7.3.3 in fact reports an RM correlation for the host ("Node") stratum *against a simulation oracle* ($I_{\text{comp}}$), which contradicts the premise that hosts cannot be simulated.
2. **"Robustness to operational parameter drift."** No experiment perturbs or removes rates, payloads or queue limits. The predictor's own edge weight $w(t)$ (Eq. 2) *consumes* payload size and frequency, so it depends on the very parameters it is said to be robust to missing.
3. **"Sub-second CI with incremental caching."** Not implemented (see above).

Meanwhile the target $I^*$ is a deterministic BFS functional that is 2–18× cheaper than feature extraction. A practitioner could simply compute the label. The authors acknowledge this in §4.4 and §8.3. It still has to be confronted head-on in the framing: as written, the paper's own RQ5 answers RQ1's motivation in the negative.

**Recommended:** retarget RQ1–RQ2 on $I_{\text{dyn}}$, which the authors already identify as their "first intended experiment", *before* submission. This is the single change that would give the learned model a non-trivial task. At minimum, add a missing-parameter robustness experiment.

### M5. The synchronous-RPC "ingestion boundary" is post hoc, and its mechanism is not what the oracle models.
§7.4 explains the negative active-stratum ρ on Online Boutique and Train-Ticket by "backward timeout cascades" [48]. The labels on those systems, however, come from the *same forward BFS reachability oracle* $I^*$ used everywhere, and that oracle models no timeouts, retries or backward propagation. Whatever explains the inversion, it cannot be a mismatch between the model's directional bias and the "physical failure flow". In this experiment the failure flow is defined by the simulator, and it is forward in every case.

The input graphs do not support the explanation either. The replication code shows that the two "synchronous" systems are hand-authored *publish–subscribe* models: `RealWorldAdapter.create_cloud_microservices_topology` and `create_trainticket_microservices_topology` in `saag/adapters/realworld_adapter.py`.
- The "Online Boutique" model has 22 applications, 20 topics, and Kafka, RabbitMQ, Redis and NATS brokers. The real Online Boutique is roughly eleven gRPC services with no message broker.
- The Train-Ticket model treats the Eureka discovery server as a broker.

No synchronous edge exists in either graph, so neither the model's input nor its labels contain the call-tree semantics the explanation invokes. With n = 2, a single coder, and no inter-coder agreement (§8.3), this boundary should be presented as a hypothesis. It should not be promoted to the paper's second "core claim".

### M6. Internal inconsistencies in reported quantities.
Each of these undermines confidence in a paper whose selling point is mechanical reconciliation (415 checked table values).
- **$\rho_s = -0.126$** is quoted in §1.3 and §8.1 as the justification for the Dual-Engine protocol. §7.2.1 states that the per-fold $\hat\sigma$ coefficients "are withheld… and no coefficient is quoted for them anywhere in this paper."
- **Is $I^*$ deterministic?** §4.3 says it "has zero seed-to-seed variance" and Table 3 lists it as "Deterministic". §7.1 reports a five-seed test–retest of 0.811–1.000 (median 0.982). Table 12 times a "five-seed" sweep of it, and cross-seed top-K Jaccard falls to 0.370. It cannot be both. If seeds enter the generator or the ladder, say where. If not, the label-noise ceiling is meaningless.
- **Online Boutique:** HGT-QoS full-population ρ is 0.710 in Table 9 but 0.769 in §8.1.
- **Table 6 caption:** says capacity and directionality "remain unmatched confounds **that Table 8 controls for**". §7.2 and §8.4 state that they are *not* controlled.
- **Rule 5 weight:** Table 2 gives the harmonic mean $H(w_V(\text{app}), w_V(\text{lib}))$. §6.2.1 says the Topo projection uses "the median topic weight".
- **Top-K Jaccard between $I_{\text{dyn}}$ and $I^*$:** §7.3.2 gives 0.370; §8.3 gives "0.27–0.37".
- **"Pre-registered" vs. "registered":** §6.3 insists on the latter as "not cosmetic", yet Table 6's caption and §6.3's first paragraph say "pre-registered comparator/family".
- **Table 13, "Relational heterogeneity":** "Typing is essential…" contradicts the substitution finding and the non-significant typed-vs-untyped contrast when QoS is present.

### M7. Reproducibility is disclosed as fragile, which weakens the learned results.
§1.5 and §8.3 report that learned fold means shift by up to 0.172 between the two most recent sweeps, which is twice the RQ1 margin. The same sections report a PyG device-placement bug, stale-checkpoint artifacts, and five artifacts in the deposit with no provenance block. The candour is commendable. The consequence, though, is that the magnitudes of every learned number are, in the authors' words, "pinned to these artifacts". Before submission:
- run the final sweep from a single tagged commit with deterministic CUDA settings (`torch.use_deterministic_algorithms`) or on CPU;
- report run-to-run variance across at least two full re-runs;
- ensure every artifact carries provenance.

### M8. Evaluation validity: synthetic single-generator corpus and small real-world sample.
- All 12 training and testing folds, including the "ATM case study", come from one generator. Their code metrics (LOC, cyclomatic complexity, LCOM) are synthetic as well. LOSO across scenarios from the same generator is closer to in-distribution than the "distribution shift" language implies. Report generator-parameter diversity across folds, or add a second, independently written generator.
- The five "real-world" systems are small ($|V_{\text{app}}| = 22$–$41$) and are not transcriptions. They are architecture models hand-authored in code by one author, "based on" public descriptions, with invented brokers, QoS profiles, code metrics and host specifications (see M5). The manuscript's "transcribed from public repositories" overstates their fidelity. It should describe them as models, list the deviations from each original system, and temper RQ4 accordingly. The claimed "Architecture-as-Code manifest" ingestion is never demonstrated on real manifests (Kubernetes, ROS 2 launch files, Kafka configs). Either provide an automated parser evaluated on real artifacts, or reframe the input as "architecture models".
- Wilcoxon over LOSO folds with overlapping training sets gives anti-conservative p-values, which the authors acknowledge. Consider a corrected resampled test (Nadeau–Bengio) or a permutation test at the scenario level for the confirmatory contrasts.

### M9. No external baseline, and a thin link to the prior literature.
Every comparator is either the authors' own closed-form score or an ablation of their own model (acknowledged in §8.4). For a Q1 venue, at least one published learned criticality ranker (DrBC or FINDER, both with public code) should be reproduced, and so should one architecture-based reliability model (for example, a Cheung-style Markov model on the same graphs). The related-work section also omits the literature on:
- change-impact and dependency analysis in microservices and service meshes, beyond RCA;
- learning-to-rank for software defect and failure prediction;
- recent GNN work on software-architecture graphs.

### M10. Length and verbosity: the manuscript reads as a revision ledger.
The PDF may fit within 35 pages, but the prose carries heavy redundancy:
- The +0.085 / p = 0.151 result is restated about eight times.
- The capacity/directionality confound is described in full in §6.2.1, §7.2, Table 6, Table 8, §8.3 and §8.4.
- The 79.3 s / 4.5 s comparison appears in the Abstract, §1.1, §1.5, §7.5, §7.5.1, §8.2 and §9.

The text is also full of meta-commentary addressed to earlier reviewers ("Earlier versions of this manuscript corrected two overlapping families…", "We withdraw the efficiency claim", "Two earlier pairings are superseded rather than reconciled", "Hence, this is not inertia"). That material belongs in a response letter, not a first-round JSS submission.

Other sections that can be cut or merged:
- §4.2's general loss envelope (Eq. 7), which describes terms that are never active;
- the disabled Relationship-Criticality head;
- §4.2.2 (domain reweighting), whose promised evaluation in §7.3 never appears;
- the Rule-6 statistics quoted from an unpublished companion study;
- the §8.4 single-paragraph wall.

Consolidate each caveat once in §8.3 and cross-reference it elsewhere. A 25–30% reduction is realistic and would sharpen the paper considerably.

## 4. Minor Comments

1. **Abstract length:** 262 words, over the Guide's 250-word limit. It also relies on non-standard abbreviations (`HGT-QoS`, Δρ, ρ) that the Guide asks authors to avoid or define.
2. **"F1@K"** is, by the authors' own statement, top-K overlap (P = R = F1). Rename the column (Overlap@K or Precision@K) in Tables 6 and 9 and in the text, rather than keeping the misleading label with a disclaimer.
3. **The notation table after Table 1** has no number or caption. The Guide requires numbered tables with captions.
4. **Terminology drift:** "Execution Host", "Node" (§7.3.3) and "host" are used interchangeably. The layer names Application/Middleware/Infrastructure/Global System (§3.3) do not match the code's `app/mw/infra/system`. "RM", "$Q(v)$", "explanation layer" and "Pathway A" are used for the same thing.
5. **§4.2.2** promises sensitivity "relative to the static RM baseline … in §7.3". No such analysis appears there.
6. **§3.3** cites MIL-STD-498 [81] for architectural layering. That is a documentation standard, so the citation is tangential. Either justify it or drop it.
7. **$I^*$ QoS ladder (§4.3):** the rationale for *increasing* feed loss by ×1.2 on `RELIABLE` topics is not given. Explain why a reliable-delivery contract amplifies measured loss.
8. **Eq. 5:** the Reliability head is supervised on $I^*$ and the Maintainability head is unsupervised, so neither is a reliability or maintainability head in any meaningful sense. Rename them (for example "auxiliary head") or remove them.
9. **§1.1** reports results (79.3 s, 2–18×, 56 ms) in the Motivation. Move them to §7 and §8.
10. **Table 11:** the forward pass at |V| = 249 (26.5 ms) is slower than at 499 (16.4 ms), which is warm-up noise. Either discard warm-up iterations or explain.
11. **§7.1 Insight 4:** "Enlarging the corpus will not resolve this" is speculation. More folds change the Wilcoxon null distribution. Soften it.
12. **Training cost is absent from RQ5:** GPU type, total training hours and energy for 12 folds × 5 seeds × 6 learned arms. For a special issue with a sustainability theme, this is the dominant energy term and should be reported. §8.2 estimates only inference and gate energy, at a nominal 28 W.
13. **Figures:** only Figure 1 is referenced in the main text, while `Figure_2` and `Figure_3` exist in `latex/figures/` and the submission package. Remove the unused files or reference them. The Guide requires figures to be cited in order.
14. **Table 1** mixes entity and edge taxonomies in one table with a mid-table header row. Split it into two tables for readability and accessibility.
15. **§7.3.3 attention analysis:** it is one scenario (ATM), first layer only, with a spread "driven substantially by destination in-degree artifacts". Either move it to the supplement or drop the claim in §2.4 that HGT "shows which architectural relations spread the cascade".
16. **References:** [47] and [54] are arXiv preprints. Check for published versions. [19] is vendor documentation; cite a peer-reviewed source where possible. For [4] and [19], the "accessed 9 September 2026" dates should be verified for consistency with the submission date.
17. **Language:** there are several awkward constructions ("projects implicit runtime interactions throughout a QoS-weighted logical dependency layer", "manifest significant improvement", "making sure that every fold carries"). Mixed register runs through the text, from "ruinous global outages" to hedged statistical prose. A professional language edit is advisable.
18. **Declarations:** the Generative-AI declaration is present and follows the required heading, and CRediT, competing-interest, funding and data-availability statements are present, so these are compliant. The Data Availability statement should drop "Five artifacts carry no provenance block"; fix the artifacts instead.
19. **Highlights:** all five bullets are within 85 characters, so they are compliant. Highlight 1, however, repeats the over-framing of M3 ("forecasts cascade failure blast radii") and should state the negative result directly.
20. **Keywords:** "Heterogeneous graph neural networks" and "distributed systems dependability" are multi-word. That is acceptable, but consider adding "publish–subscribe" and "negative results" as single indexing terms, and staying within 7 keywords.

## 5. Recommendation

**Major Revision.**

**Justification.** The problem is relevant to the special issue. The statistical reporting is more transparent than most submissions, and the negative findings (parity with a closed-form baseline; static gating costing more than simulation) are genuinely useful to the community. The paper cannot be accepted in its current form, for four reasons:
1. The headline scientific claim — typing and QoS as substitutes — rests on a factorial whose two rows receive different edge channels (1-D vs 16-D) at a 15.4× capacity gap (M1). The floor arm it is contrasted against is itself unstable (M2).
2. The Abstract, Introduction and Conclusion claim transfer, triage reliability, and sub-second gating that §7 explicitly reports as unresolved, unevaluated or unimplemented (M3).
3. The motivation for learning over simulation is contradicted by the paper's own cost and label-construction evidence (M4, M5).
4. Multiple quantitative inconsistencies remain (M6), and the manuscript is heavily padded with revision-history prose (M10).

Rejection is not recommended because the corrective experiments are within reach of the existing infrastructure:
- the capacity- and channel-matched 2×2 (GAT-N-C, GAT-N-QoS16-C, HGT, HGT-QoS);
- the four already-implemented control arms;
- retargeting on $I_{\text{dyn}}$;
- one published baseline.

The corrections to the framing are straightforward. If those experiments are run and the paper is re-framed honestly around what they show, this could become a solid JSS contribution on the empirical limits of graph learning for pre-deployment dependability analysis.

## Revision 2 (2026-09-23): success-led framing and SaG-Hybrid

- **Framing.** The paper now leads with what SaG achieves:
  - the QoS-aware projection beats unweighted centrality on 12/12 folds (+0.204);
  - typed learning beats untyped GNNs (+0.234, 12/12);
  - zero-shot transfer to the five system models (ρ 0.760 vs 0.51–0.53);
  - critical-set identification (PR-AUC 0.713 vs ≈0.5).

  Topo-QoS is presented as SaG's own closed-form engine. The registered HGT-QoS vs Topo-QoS contrast (n.s.) and the hand-authored nature of the system models are each still reported, once.
- **New method: SaG-Hybrid.** HGT-QoS with the rank-normalised Topo-QoS score as an extra input, learning a logit correction to it. It was registered as Amendment 5 before any run.
