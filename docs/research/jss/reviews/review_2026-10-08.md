# Referee Report — Journal of Systems and Software
## Special Issue: AI Techniques for Performance, Reliability, and Sustainability of Modern Software Systems

**Manuscript:** *Software-as-a-Graph: When Does Graph Learning Improve Cascade-Impact Ranking in Publish–Subscribe Systems?*
**Reviewed version:** `docs/research/jss/manuscript.md` at `main` 30811aa1 (28 pp. compiled), with `supplementary.md` (48 pp., 74 tables)
**Date:** 2026-10-08

---

## 1. Summary

The paper asks whether graph learning adds value beyond analytical dependency analysis when ranking publish–subscribe Applications by simulated cascading-failure impact before deployment. It derives an explicit `DEPENDS_ON` graph from deployment manifests and compares training-free, hybrid, and GNN/GBM rankers. The evaluation uses leave-one-scenario-out validation over twelve synthetic architectures, three simulators (reachability, queue-flow, composite), and zero-shot transfer to five hand-authored open-source system models. Its main contribution is a carefully controlled negative result: the derived dependency representation, not model complexity, carries the signal. Simple first-order references (direct-dependent count ρ = 0.764; a rate-weighted formula ρ = 0.830 on the expensive queue-flow simulator) are matched but never significantly exceeded by learned models. The paper also proposes a "reference criterion" to tell a ranking that restates a simulator's rule apart from one that predicts it.

## 2. Overall Impression and Assessment

**Originality: moderate.**
- The publish–subscribe dependency projection extends the authors' RASSE paper [30].
- The reference criterion makes operational a concern that the shortcut-learning and simulation-validation literatures already state.
- The empirical question, whether learning beats aligned analytical baselines on simulator-labeled cascade ranking, is timely for this special issue.

**Significance: potentially useful, but bounded more tightly than the framing admits.**
- JSS welcomes negative results, and "use afferent coupling / a rate-weighted first-order formula before training a GNN" is actionable.
- However, all three oracles are first-order by construction. A first-order formula matching a first-order simulator is close to what one would predict a priori. The study therefore cannot answer the question its title asks.

**Methodological soundness: unusually transparent, but the evidential basis is weak.**
- I commend:
  - the direction control (F8) and the feature-stripping control (F11);
  - the Spearman–Brown reliability ceiling on I_dyn;
  - the honest reporting of an implementation defect in the baseline;
  - the node-order permutation flag;
  - the mechanical reconciliation of 1,817 figures against artifacts.
- Against this, both confirmatory contrasts are null. Every headline result is "registered secondary" (committed *after* the primary null was known) or exploratory, across seventeen protocol amendments.
- The learning arm is untuned and heavily over-parameterized for the data available.
- No label comes from a real system, and no topology comes from a real manifest.

**Suitability for a Q1 journal: not yet.**
- The controlled-comparison design and the transparency are of Q1 quality.
- The benchmark does not yet give learning a fair chance to succeed. The confirmatory status of the claims can be repaired cheaply (the authors say so themselves) but has not been.
- The manuscript reads more like a revision ledger than a paper.

## 3. Major Comments

### M1. The benchmark cannot answer the title question, and the framing overclaims

- §4.3 states that I_dyn "by construction … propagates no failure beyond one hop". §7.4 states that all three oracles "are dominated by first-order propagation by construction".
- §7.5 item 3 concedes that non-first-order regimes are "the only ones in which this study's title question could receive a positive answer".
- Yet the title asks *when* learning improves ranking. Contribution 3 claims to be "defining the conditions under which non-first-order dynamics might benefit from learning". No such condition is defined or tested.
- The central queue-flow finding is close to tautological. A one-hop rate-weighted formula (Eq. 7) approximates a simulator whose propagation is one-hop and rate-driven.
- §1.2 calls I_dyn "a genuine surrogate-modeling problem". But its 12.7 CPU-hours reflect a discrete-event simulation used to compute a one-hop effect, not intrinsic difficulty.

**Requested.** Either:
- (a) add at least one non-first-order oracle. The SimPy simulator already exists, so publisher blocking on full queues, finite broker capacity, or retry amplification is an incremental change, and §7.2 names exactly these mechanisms. Or:
- (b) retitle and reframe the paper as a scoped benchmark on first-order propagation, and remove the "defining the conditions" language from Contribution 3 and the Conclusion.

Option (a) would turn a bounded negative result into a genuinely informative one.

### M2. The representation and the primary oracle share the same propagation assumption (construct validity)

- Rule 1 (subscriber → publisher) and I*'s first propagation wave encode the same author-specified assumption about how failures spread. Remark 1 shows that `InDeg` *equals* the first-wave support on all seventeen graphs.
- "Representation mattered more than model complexity" is therefore partly true by design. The `DEPENDS_ON` projection is, in effect, the oracle's propagation graph.
- The raw-multigraph learners are additionally handicapped because every relation points away from Applications (§3.4).
- The `-R` and `-min` controls are good and remove the two most obvious explanations. They do not remove this one.
- The value of the derivation is also measured only against I* (+0.058 for Reach with Rule 5). I* itself propagates through libraries.

**Requested.**
- State explicitly that the derivation's advantage is conditional on real propagation following Rules 1 and 5, which is untested.
- Phrase the representation claim as "a representation aligned with the oracle's propagation mechanism".
- Discuss what would happen under an oracle whose propagation direction or mechanism differs from Rule 1 (e.g., backpressure from subscriber to publisher).

### M3. Inferential status: everything that matters is post hoc, and the cheap fix has not been done

- The two co-primary contrasts (HGT-QoS and HGT vs. Topo-QoS) are null.
- The "registered secondary" arms were committed after the primary null was known. Their Holm families were defined at the same time.
- The reference criterion, including the "closed set" of admissible simplifications S1–S5, was defined *after* the dependency counts performed strongly (§4.4). The closed set classifies exactly the high-scoring rankings as references, and Topo-QoS and every learner as predictors. A skeptical reader will see a rule shaped to fit the outcome.
- §7.5 item 1 says a confirmation corpus is "the cheapest way to make the present headline findings confirmatory", because topologies regenerate exactly and I* labels take seconds.

**Requested (essential).**
- Generate a fresh corpus after freezing the analysis scripts. Ideally use different generator seeds and at least partly different generator parameters.
- Re-test the claims the paper's narrative rests on: GAT-P-QoS vs. GAT-QoS-R (F8), the F11 stripped contrast, GAT-P-QoS vs. InDeg, and Eq. 7 vs. GBM→dyn (F12).
- Apply the reference criterion, as stated, to that corpus before seeing results.
- Given the authors' own cost estimates, I see no justification for submitting without this.

### M4. The learned arm is not a fair or well-specified competitor

1. **No tuning.** One fixed configuration replaced the registered nested selection, which is a plan deviation. Running that selection later moved the learners by ±0.055 (§6.2), the same size as many reported contrasts.
2. **Over-parameterization.** About 430,000 parameters per GNN are trained on about 1,000–1,300 labeled nodes from eleven graphs. The authors acknowledge this biases the comparison toward the null (§7.4). No learning curve was run, although "the generator makes these free" (§7.5). Please run learning curves over the number of training architectures and over model size.
3. **Tie-order leakage in ListMLE.**
   - About 31% of labels are tied at zero, and ties enter π in generator creation order.
   - The published GAT-P-QoS (0.748) lies *above all three* permutation runs (0.712, 0.740, 0.734).
   - The text reads this as "variance rather than a favorable published order". Three of three permutations below the published value cannot support that conclusion; the data cannot tell the two apart.
   - At the permutation mean (0.729), the gap to InDeg doubles to 0.035.
   - Please adopt a tie-aware listwise loss (Plackett–Luce with ties, LambdaLoss, or approxNDCG) and report the permutation-averaged value as the headline.
4. **Unstable HGT-P-QoS.** Seeds anti-correlated with the oracle are retained in the means (seed SD 0.254). The authors correctly call this an optimization failure, but it then cannot inform typing at all. Either tune it or drop it from the main tables.
5. **Missing queue-flow GNN.** The GNN trained on I_dyn received no rates and "did not fit its target" (§6.1). The learned-vs-analytical comparison on the expensive oracle therefore rests on a single GBM. A GNN given the declared rates is the obvious missing arm.
6. **Reproducibility drift.** "Across revision cycles and compute devices, a learned cell drifted by up to 0.172" (§7.4). That is larger than every effect in the paper. Pairing contrasts within one sweep controls it statistically, but the cause needs to be diagnosed and reported.
7. **Architecture choice.**
   - Sum aggregation (GIN) beats attention whenever degree features are removed (F2, F11).
   - A featureless GIN reaches 0.719.
   - The paper itself argues that counting is the relevant inductive bias.
   - The main learned ranker should arguably be a sum-aggregation model.
   - Simple-HGN, which Lv et al. [80] propose as the well-tuned heterogeneous baseline, and RGCN are also missing.

### M5. The comparator choice inflates the "learning beats the baseline" message

- The registered comparator Topo-QoS has a defect (AP term identically zero), so it is pure QoS-weighted betweenness. The authors concede that "afferent coupling would have been the natural comparator" (§5.2).
- Yet the abstract, §1.3, highlight 5 ("hybrids beat the baseline") and the Conclusion lead with gains against this weak comparator: +0.103/+0.130, and 0.81 vs. 0.53 zero-shot.
- The paper then shows that:
  - the hybrids do not beat their own base learners;
  - "the gain belongs to the baseline's weakness" (§7.2);
  - given InDeg as a prior, the hybrids reproduce InDeg (F10).

**Requested.**
- Re-anchor the abstract, highlights and conclusions on the strongest training-free comparator.
- Keep the registered Topo-QoS contrasts, but present them as what they are: a test against a weak, defective baseline.
- Describe the defect (including the `ap_c_score` dictionary key) in one footnote and the supplement, not in four places in the main text.

### M6. Unsupported or internally contradictory statements

1. **"No learned model exceeded the references."** §6.1 (l. 282) says "no learned model exceeded the references derived from the same dependencies (0.764–0.808, against at most 0.748)". §1.3 makes the same claim.
   - The quoted range silently omits Reach (0.732), which GAT-P-QoS (0.748) exceeds.
   - The five-seed ensemble (0.772, Table 6) also exceeds InDeg (0.764).
   - Write "none *significantly* exceeded", and quote the full reference range.
2. **Active-stratum heuristics.** §7.3 (l. 444) says that for active discrimination "transitive or direct dependent counts remain the superior structural heuristics".
   - Table 8 gives InDeg ρ>0 = 0.321, below GAT-P-QoS at 0.342.
   - The same sentence says "simple degree heuristics" do not discriminate reliably.
   - Only Reach (0.871) does.
3. **RQ3 and the "unfamiliar topology" row of Table 10.** These recommend Reach and InDeg for unfamiliar topologies. Reach restates I*'s untruncated rule, and I* runs in under a second on any manifest; row 1 of the same table says "Run I* directly".
   - Zero-shot transfer of an I* predictor therefore has no practical use case.
   - RQ3 would be meaningful on I_dyn, where a surrogate is needed. Please either run zero-shot transfer against I_dyn or justify RQ3's practical motivation.
4. **Reference criterion is not applied consistently.**
   - Reach is described as "the support of the untruncated cascade". That is not an order-k truncation for any finite k, so it falls outside the definition as written.
   - Eq. 7 is asserted to be T_1(I_dyn), but I_dyn is a discrete-event simulation without "waves". The mapping needs a derivation (supplement), not an assertion.
5. **Ceiling arithmetic.** In §4.3 and §6.1, "leaving at most 0.06–0.17" combines fold-wise ceilings with a pooled mean ρ; compute the gap per fold. The sentence "every ranker … stays below r, and therefore below √r" is muddled. Being below r is the stronger statement, and it is not the ceiling argument.
6. **Break-even claim.** §6.4: "repays that only after replacing hundreds of expensive simulator evaluations" has no unit.
   - Per architecture labeling (≈30 Wh), the 0.58 kWh break-even is about 20 architectures.
   - Per fault run and seed (≈0.054 Wh), it is about 10⁴.
   - Please state the unit and the arithmetic.

### M7. External validity and practical significance are thin for a special issue on modern systems

- No topology was extracted from a real manifest, and no label comes from an observed failure.
- The five system models are written by a single modeler, and two are RPC systems recast as pub-sub.
- All twelve folds come from one generator family.
- The practical protocol (Tier 1/Tier 2 triage) is unvalidated. By the paper's own figures, 80% recall requires flagging 40–45% of Applications.

**Requested, in order of cost.**
1. Execute the second-modeler protocol that the package already supports (`reproduce/model_agreement.py`).
2. Extract at least one model automatically from real artifacts, e.g., Autoware launch files via HAROS/ROSDiscover, or the Train-Ticket compose/Kubernetes manifests, and report how it differs from the hand-authored version.
3. Run a small fault-injection sanity check on one deployable system (Train-Ticket is cited [31]) to show that I* or I_dyn rank at least the top few components plausibly.

Even a modest result from (3) would considerably raise the paper's significance.

### M8. Literature gaps

1. **Learned influential-spreader identification trained on SIR-simulation labels.** This is the closest methodological analog to the circularity problem, and that literature routinely finds degree and k-shell to be strong baselines. Examples: Zhao et al., *InfGCN*, Neurocomputing 2020; Yu et al., *Identifying critical nodes in complex networks via graph convolutional networks*, Knowledge-Based Systems 2020. The reference criterion should be positioned against this work, not only against Kitsak et al. [53].
2. **GNN surrogates of network and queueing simulators.** A key example is RouteNet (Rusek et al., SOSR 2019 / IEEE JSAC 2020), where learned models beat analytical queueing approximations precisely in non-first-order regimes. This directly informs M1 and the paper's open question.
3. **Recency.** Only a handful of references are from 2024–2026, and several of those are standards, access dates, or self-citations. Recent GNN work on microservice reliability, RCA and dependency analysis should be surveyed for a 2026 special issue on AI for reliability.
4. **Heterogeneous GNN baselines.** Simple-HGN and RGCN should at least be discussed (see M4.7).

*(Please verify these citations; they are given as pointers.)*

### M9. The writing is verbose, defensive, and laden with internal jargon

- The same caveats recur throughout:
  - "first-order" appears 46 times;
  - "exploratory" appears 22 times;
  - "not significantly different" appears 9 times;
  - the bimodal-sparsity statement appears four times (§§5.1, 6.3, 7.3, 7.4).
- The abstract, §1.3 and the Conclusion largely triplicate each other.
- Internal project vocabulary leaks into the text:
  - F1–F13 arm codes and amendment numbers;
  - "Validate-stage", "S+Q design", "stage-1 grid";
  - `ap_c_score` and `PYTHONHASHSEED`;
  - "defined independently of any target vector", which only makes sense given the revision history.
- The ranker suffix system (`-P`, `-R`, `-deg`, `-min`, `-const`, `-perm`, `-AP`, `→dyn`) imposes a heavy cognitive load.

**Suggested.**
- Replace the scattered hedges with one "Status of evidence" box (confirmatory / secondary / exploratory) and refer to it.
- Move the four unevaluated rules (2, 3, 4, 6) out of main-text Table 2.
- Move the QoS weighting machinery (power mean, library amplification, AHP), which the paper itself shows is inert, to the supplement.
- Remove the unevaluated explanation layer from Figure 1.
- Cut §1.3 to a short paragraph. The abstract is exactly 250 words and could lose the dangling clause "where the registered co-primary contrasts were null".

## 4. Minor Comments

1. **Title.** "When does…" promises a characterization the paper does not provide (M1). Something like *"Does Graph Learning Add Value Beyond Dependency Analysis for First-Order Cascade-Impact Ranking in Publish–Subscribe Systems?"* would be accurate.
2. **Highlights.** All five meet the JSS limits (3–5 bullets, ≤85 characters; the longest is 82). However:
   - Highlight 3 calls InDeg a "baseline", although the paper calls it a reference.
   - Highlight 5 promotes the hybrid-vs-baseline result, which §7.2 calls weak evidence.
3. **Keywords.** "Reliability" and "dependability" are redundant. Consider "negative results", "surrogate modeling" or "simulation-based evaluation".
4. **§3.1.** μ* (the Morris elementary-effects statistic) is used without definition.
5. **Remark 1.** "The in-degree *feature* … also follows `USES` links". USES runs App → Library, so it is unclear how it contributes to an Application's in-degree. The two quantities differ for 940 of 1,321 Applications (71%), which matters for interpreting F1. Define the feature precisely.
6. **§4.2.** The auxiliary target I*_R ("the reliability component the same simulator run records") is undefined in the main text.
7. **§4.3, I* constants.** The threshold 0.2, wave decay 0.15, floor 0.25 and severity ladder 1.2/1.15/1.05 are arbitrary. Point to a sensitivity analysis or provide one.
8. **§5.3, Nadeau–Bengio.** The correction is derived for repeated random train/test resampling, not LOSO; justify its use. The phrase "taking Applications as the unit instead" is unclear.
9. **Table 8.** Percentile bootstrap CIs over n = 5 systems (126 distinct resamples) are not meaningful. Report min–max instead.
10. **Table 6.**
    - It mixes seed-ensemble rows with mean-of-per-seed rows. Split the table or harmonize the protocol.
    - Raw total degree (0.719), the Table 10 recommendation for I_comp, should be a row, not a caption note.
11. **Duplication.** Tables 5 and 6 duplicate the I* values, and Table 10 restates numbers given elsewhere. The JSS guide asks that tables not duplicate results.
12. **§6.4 / §7.4, energy.** Energy is a nameplate-TDP × wall-clock estimate. For a special issue with a sustainability strand, either measure it (RAPL via a permitted environment, CodeCarbon, or an external meter) or reduce energy claims to a footnote. The break-even argument survives either way.
13. **§7.5, last paragraph.** It duplicates the Data Availability statement and differs from the rest of the paper in style (em dashes).
14. **Typography.**
    - l. 422: "plus model training ( 0.22 kWh)" has a stray space.
    - Figure 5 caption: "1, 321".
    - Figure 1 caption: "Gstructural/Ganalysis" lacks subscripts.
15. **Supplementary material.**
    - At 48 pages and 74 tables, it exceeds the main paper.
    - §S3 contains stale material on an "RM" composite and a λ shrinkage parameter ("expert elicitation makes the ranking worse"). It cites "Section 5.2 of the main manuscript", which does not mention RM, and RM is not defined anywhere in the main text.
    - Prune the supplement to material the main text actually references.
16. **Submission metadata.** `latex/LENGTH_JUSTIFICATION.md` states 27 pages and 100 references. The compiled PDF has 28 pages and the reference list 98 entries.
17. **Data availability.** The GitHub link is pinned to commit 56d9bff8, which exists on `main`; good. I could not verify that the Zenodo DOI (10.5281/zenodo.23045204) resolves; please confirm that it is public before submission.
18. **Declaration of generative AI.** It is correctly placed and worded per the guide.

## 5. Recommendation

**Major Revision.**

**Justification.** The paper asks a worthwhile question and reports a carefully controlled, transparently documented negative result of the kind JSS explicitly welcomes. Its controls (F8, F11, F12), its reliability ceiling and its reconciliation pipeline are better than most of the literature. It is not acceptable in its current form, for three reasons:

1. **The design gives learning no fair chance.** All three oracles are first-order by construction (M1), and the primary oracle shares its propagation assumption with the representation being credited (M2). The study therefore cannot answer the question in its title.
2. **The headline claims are exploratory or post hoc** after seventeen amendments (M3), and the learned arm is untuned, over-parameterized and affected by tie-order leakage (M4). Both problems can be fixed cheaply, by the authors' own account.
3. **Real-world grounding is absent** (M7), and the presentation overstates gains against a defective baseline (M5) while being substantially more verbose than necessary (M9).

**Essential for the next round:**
- the confirmation corpus (M3);
- a tie-aware loss and learning curves (M4.2–3);
- either a non-first-order oracle or a retitled, explicitly scoped framing (M1);
- the corrections listed in M6.

**Strongly recommended:**
- a GNN given declared rates on I_dyn (M4.5);
- the second-modeler check, plus at least one automatically extracted real topology (M7);
- substantial condensation of the text (M9).

If the confirmation corpus reproduces the main secondary findings, and the scope is either widened to a non-first-order oracle or narrowed honestly in the title and claims, I expect this to become a solid, well-cited negative-result contribution.
