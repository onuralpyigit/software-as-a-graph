## 1. Summary

The manuscript proposes Software-as-a-Graph (SaG). SaG converts publish–subscribe architecture descriptions into a typed multigraph and derives from it a QoS-weighted `DEPENDS_ON` projection (six rules). It uses this projection to rank Applications by simulated cascading-failure impact. It compares a training-free engine (QoS-weighted betweenness, `Topo-QoS`), capacity-matched GNNs (HGT/GAT, with and without a 16-D QoS edge encoding), and two "hybrid" GNNs that learn a logit correction to the closed-form score. An AHP-weighted ISO/IEC 25010 profile is offered as an explanation layer. Under 12-fold leave-one-scenario-out (LOSO) cross-validation on one family of synthetic generators, the authors report four main results:

- QoS weighting lifts betweenness from ρ = 0.349 to 0.553.
- The hybrids reach ρ = 0.657/0.683 and are the only engines that significantly beat `Topo-QoS`.
- Relation-typed parameters add nothing at matched capacity.
- Learned engines transfer zero-shot to five hand-authored system models at ρ = 0.760–0.805.

## 2. Overall Impression & Assessment

**Originality — moderate.** The main new element is the representation: typed pub-sub entities, QoS-weighted derived dependencies, and the distinction between sequential cascades and simultaneous blasts. Heterogeneous GNNs, ListMLE ranking losses, residual "prior + learned correction" hybrids and AHP composites are all established techniques. The paper's own matched control (RQ2) removes the heterogeneous-GNN novelty that earlier drafts leaned on.

**Significance — limited by the construct.** The target is a simulator, and that simulator uses only the manifest the predictors read. §7.4 and S28 show that it runs 2–18× *faster* than SaG's own feature extraction. So the paper still has to show why a learned approximation of this oracle is useful (M1). Without real-incident labels, a behavioural oracle (I_dyn) as the training target, or an independently modelled real system, the practical significance for dependability engineering is unproven.

**Methodological soundness — mixed.** On transparency and reporting discipline the paper is exceptional, beyond what JSS usually receives:

- a registered analysis plan with a dated amendment log;
- an omnibus Holm correction;
- active-stratum re-analysis;
- label test–retest ceilings;
- a withdrawn-claims record;
- mechanical reconciliation of 511 reported figures against artifacts;
- byte-identical corpus regeneration.

I commend this without reservation. The inferential core, however, has five problems:

- The oracle is described inaccurately, and it shares the predictor's dependency semantics (M2).
- The generator builds in the QoS–topology coupling that the central thesis credits to "declared QoS contracts" (M3).
- The hybrids were designed after the per-fold complementarity was seen, and they are not better than their own learned engines (M4).
- Several headline sentences go beyond non-significant or n = 5 evidence (M5, M6).
- Learned results drift across devices by as much as the effects claimed (M8).

**Suitability for a Q1 venue / this special issue.** The topic fits the special issue's reliability strand ("AI for Reliability and Dependability Analysis"). The sustainability strand is touched only by upper-bound energy arithmetic (§8.1), and the performance strand not at all. The paper is within the Guide's recommended length: 22 single-column pages plus a 25-page supplement. The abstract (238 words), highlights (5 bullets, 80–84 characters), keywords (7), CRediT, competing interests, data availability and the generative-AI declaration are all present and broadly compliant (see Minor comments for the exceptions). In its current form I do not think the evidence supports the abstract's central sentence, *"Declaring QoS contracts in architecture models thus makes cascading-failure risk measurable, and explainable, before deployment."*

## 3. Major Comments

### M1. The paper does not establish why a learned surrogate of a cheap, same-input oracle is useful.

The primary label I*(v) is computed from the architecture model alone: topology, QoS fields and publication rates. It needs no runtime telemetry. The authors' own measurements (§7.4, Supplementary S28, Table S-gate_ratio) show that the full five-seed labelling sweep takes 0.08–4.49 s per scenario. SaG's analysis gate takes 0.16–79.3 s, 2.0–17.7× slower on every scenario. §7.4 concedes that "direct simulation is therefore faster wherever its parameters are available". The paper then argues that SaG's advantage is "avoiding staging infrastructure", but the in-process oracle needs no staging infrastructure either. As written, the predictive pathway approximates a function that the practitioner could compute exactly, more cheaply, from the same inputs. It does so with full-population ρ ≈ 0.62–0.68, and ρ ≈ 0.28–0.36 on components that actually propagate failures (S25).

For the predictive contribution to stand, the authors need at least one of the following:

- **(a) A setting where the oracle cannot be run but SaG can.** Examples: partially specified manifests with missing rates or QoS, edge-level ranking, or design-space search where thousands of candidate edits must be scored. Then measure it.
- **(b) Evidence that the learned engines predict something beyond I\*.** For example, train on I* and evaluate on I_dyn. That experiment is listed as "the next experiment" in §8.2 and as future work (3). It is central, not optional.
- **(c) A reframing of the paper.** Make the contribution the representation, the closed-form engine and the explanation layer, and present the GNNs as an empirical study of what learning adds.

In the same vein, "Neural inference takes milliseconds" (abstract, highlight 5, §9) is technically true but misleading. End-to-end scoring of an unseen 2,000-component architecture takes about four minutes (§7.4), and that is the cost a CI gate pays.

### M2. The primary oracle is described inaccurately, and it shares the predictors' dependency semantics. This weakens the "input–label independence" argument.

§4.3 and Table 4 describe I* as "BFS cascade reachability + QoS ladder" in which "five seeds break ties in propagation order". The implementation (`saag/simulation/fault_injector.py`, module docstring) does something materially different:

- **Phase A.** Failure propagates *outright* along derived `app_to_lib` `DEPENDS_ON` arcs (Rule 5).
- **Phase B.** Failure propagates through rate-weighted, QoS-scaled topic feed loss. A subscriber whose loss exceeds a propagation threshold θ = 0.2 fails *stochastically*, with probability min(1, loss/θ) × depth_damp. The damping decays by 0.15 per wave to a floor of 0.25.

This is a stochastic threshold-cascade model with at least three free parameters that the main text never states. It explains why test–retest falls to 0.811 on Microservices, which tie-breaking alone would not. Three consequences follow:

1. **§4.3 and Table 4 must describe the oracle as implemented.** That means the phase structure, θ, the damping, the rate weighting, and the role of the seeds. The sensitivity to θ and damping should be reported in the main text. S3 is referenced only in passing and appears to cover only the threshold.
2. **The independence guarantee (§4.4) is procedurally true but semantically weak.** The project's own invariants require `SimulationGraph.get_dependency_edges()` to re-derive all six `DEPENDS_ON` rules and to match the predictor-side arc set exactly. Labels and features therefore embody the same hand-designed dependency semantics: Rule 5's "simultaneous blast" appears on both sides by construction, and the rate weighting mirrors `FreqNorm` in w(t). The claim that the oracle "runs only on G_structural" should be restated to acknowledge this. Agreement between the predictor and I* partly measures the self-consistency of the authors' rule set, not dependability.
3. **Missing training-free baselines.** Learned engines receive the Connectivity Degradation Index (CDI) as a feature (§3.4). CDI "removes each node and measures the resulting connectivity loss" on G_analysis, which is a node-removal simulation and a close relative of the label. The closed-form comparators do not use it. The paper also lists I_M(v), a reverse-`DEPENDS_ON` traversal, in Table 4 but never uses it. Please report, as training-free rankers under the same LOSO protocol:
   - CDI alone;
   - QoS-weighted transitive-dependent count (reverse reachability) on the projection, i.e. I_M;
   - in-degree on the reverse projection;
   - the `GBM-Feat` baseline that Amendment 3 registered specifically "to isolate whether any graph-learning margin is the aggregation or just the features". The manuscript does not report it, and S24 does not say whether it was run.

   If a weighted reachability count on the projection matches the engines, the learned contribution largely collapses. If it does not, the paper becomes much stronger. Either way the reader needs to know.

The rationale for the QoS severity ladder, which *amplifies* feed loss by ×1.2 on `RELIABLE` topics, is still not given. A reliable-delivery contract would usually be read as *reducing* the effective loss.

### M3. The synthetic generator builds in the QoS–topology coupling that the central thesis credits to "declared QoS contracts".

The generator (`tools/generation/generator.py`, `_APP_TYPE_QOS_AFFINITY` and `_qos_preferred_topics`) samples topology *conditioned on QoS*:

- gateways and controllers attach preferentially to `RELIABLE`/`HIGH` topics;
- sensors attach to `BEST_EFFORT`/`LOW` topics;
- operational criticality and hot-standby redundancy (with a secondary `RUNS_ON` edge) are assigned from a composite of QoS, app type and degree.

The oracle then up-weights `RELIABLE`/`HIGH` feeds, and the predictor weights edges by w(t) computed from the same QoS fields. So the "+0.204 from declared QoS contracts" could be the same design assumption appearing three times: in the generator, the label and the feature. The main text does not disclose this mechanism. §6.1 says only that all folds "come from one generator family".

The paper's own sensitivity results argue against a QoS-*semantics* explanation of the +0.204:

- the label is near-topological (ρ = 0.965 against a topology-only relabel, §4.3);
- no point of the (α, β, γ) simplex changes any ordering (S1);
- the QoS sub-weights have negligible Morris influence (μ* ≤ 0.025);
- with uniform topic weights, the probabilistic-union rule still up-weights pairs joined by *many* topics.

The simplest alternative hypothesis is that the gain comes from topic *multiplicity* in Rules 1–2, not from contract content. Please add:

- **(a)** `Topo` on the projection with constant w(t) but the probabilistic union retained (the multiplicity-only weighting);
- **(b)** `Topo-QoS` with QoS profiles permuted across topics within each scenario, which keeps the marginals and breaks the coupling;
- **(c)** a corpus variant generated with QoS independent of topology.

Also disclose the affinity mechanism in §6.1 and the threats to validity. Contribution 1, the title and the Conclusion's first sentence depend on this experiment.

### M4. The hybrid engines were designed after the complementarity was observed, and they are not better than their own learned engines.

Four issues:

1. **They do not beat their own learned engines.** S24 (omnibus table) shows Hybrid-HGT vs `HGT-QoS` at +0.035 (p = 0.73) and Hybrid-GAT vs `GAT-QoS` at +0.048 (p = 0.30). "Most accurate engines" (abstract, §1.4, §7.1, §9) is therefore a statement about point estimates only. The inferential pattern — learned engine n.s. vs `Topo-QoS`, hybrid significant vs `Topo-QoS`, hybrid n.s. vs learned engine — is a textbook case of "the difference between significant and not significant is not itself significant" (Gelman & Stern, 2006). Table 7's caption says p_Holm is given "vs its own learned engine", but those values do not appear in the table. They must.
2. **Anchoring reduces variance; it does not add much mean gain.** Hybrid-HGT's mean gain over `Topo-QoS` (+0.103) is close to `HGT-QoS`'s (+0.069 on CPU, +0.085 on GPU). What changes is the paired dispersion: the Δ CI shrinks from about 0.22 wide to about 0.10 wide, because the hybrid's fold errors covary with its own prior. Significance against the prior is therefore partly an artifact of evaluating against it. Please say this plainly.
3. **Adaptive design.** The plan dates from 2026-09-06. Amendment 5 (Hybrid-HGT) was registered on 09-23 and Amendment 6 (Hybrid-GAT) on 09-24, after the per-fold pattern in §7.1 ("HGT-QoS loses … where the closed-form engine is strongest") was known, and after an internal review round. Registering "before any hybrid result" controls knowledge of the *outcome*. It does not control knowledge of *which design to invent*. The omnibus Holm correction does not address this selection. The hybrids should be presented as hypothesis-generating: registered, but designed in response to observed data. §6.3 and the abstract should say so.
4. **The usage guidance is not actionable.** "Use a hybrid for architectures resembling the training corpus, a pure learned engine for substantially different systems" (§8.1, Table 11, §9) requires a practitioner to know which case applies. The paper gives no distribution-shift test, and an earlier label-free confidence signal was withdrawn. Either give an operational criterion or remove the recommendation.

### M5. RQ2's conclusions go beyond the registered evidence, and the "QoS channel" is a bundle of features.

- **The QoS effect is not significant after correction.** The registered QoS main effect is +0.073, p = 0.043, Holm p = 0.127 within its family and 0.38 omnibus (S24). Yet the abstract ("the QoS edge encoding … drives learned accuracy"), §1.4, §7.2's summary and §9 state it as established. Only the simple effect for the untyped pair (p = 0.016) is nominally significant, and it is descriptive and uncorrected by the authors' own rule.
- **"Typing adds nothing" needs an equivalence test.** The typing main effect is −0.014 with CI [−0.052, +0.023]. That is absence of evidence, not evidence of absence. Declare an equivalence margin and run TOST (e.g. Lakens, 2017), or soften the wording.
- **The "QoS channel" is several features at once.** It comprises index 0 (the QoS-derived coupling weight w_E), index 1 (a simple-path count), indices 2–8 (a relation one-hot) and indices 9–15 (the QoS fields). Per Table 6, `GAT` has no edge features while `HGT` already receives the relation one-hot. So `GAT → GAT-QoS` adds relation identity, path counts, coupling weights and QoS at once, whereas `HGT → HGT-QoS` adds only the last three. The two "QoS" simple effects therefore measure different interventions. An ablation along the channel's components is needed before the gain can be credited to QoS: relation one-hot only, then + w_E, then + path count, then + QoS dims. §7.2 already concedes that on this target the channel "acts largely as a relation-identity and coupling-strength signal". The abstract should say the same.
- **"QoS-off" arms are not QoS-free.** They still receive four QoS-weighted centralities (§3.4).
- **Registered arms not run.** Three arms registered in Amendment 2 were not run: `GAT-w`, the directionality control `HGT-QoS-U`, and the label-side sweep (S24). They should be run, or the RQ2 conclusions scoped explicitly to the arms that were.

### M6. The zero-shot transfer evidence (RQ3) is much weaker than its position in the abstract implies.

1. **Too few systems for the statistics used.** n = 5 systems, 22–41 Applications each. Percentile bootstrap intervals over five units (at most 126 distinct resamples) have poor coverage, and "intervals that do not overlap" is not a valid basis for inference. A sign test on "4 of 5 systems" gives p = 0.375.
2. **The models are not independent of the authors.** All five were hand-authored by one author, who also designed the generator and the modelling conventions. Two depart materially from their originals: Online Boutique is remodelled as a four-broker pub-sub mesh, and Train-Ticket's discovery server is modelled as a broker. The paper also mentions an "earlier, non-blind 2-layer configuration", which implies the models were inspected during development. Please state when each model was frozen (commit hash and date) relative to model development.
3. **Out-of-distribution beats in-distribution.** Zero-shot ρ (0.760–0.805) *exceeds* in-distribution LOSO ρ (0.622–0.635) for the same engines, while the closed-form scores stay flat (0.526 vs 0.553). This is a red flag that needs an explanation. Candidates include smaller graphs, a different tie structure, and modelling conventions that mirror the generator's.
4. **The active stratum is unresolved.** On the active stratum the learned result is +0.236, with a CI spanning zero, and it is non-positive on both RPC-derived models.
5. **The framing has flipped between versions.** An earlier version withdrew the zero-shot transfer claim (see [`outline.md`](../outline.md), "four claims … are withdrawn"). It is now a headline result. That is legitimate if the protocol changed, but the change and its rationale should be stated.

RQ3 should be presented as exploratory and descriptive in the abstract and highlights. The re-modelling protocol and `reproduce/model_agreement.py` are already in the package. Running a second modeller on at least two systems before resubmission would substantially raise the credibility of RQ3.

### M7. Zero-inflated labels: half of every correlation is inert-vs-active separation.

Between 21% and 52% of Applications per fold have I* = 0 (S25). Restricted to active components, every predictor keeps only about half of its ρ: `Topo-QoS` 0.280, `HGT-QoS` 0.356. The practical question is which components' failure hurts most. For that question, the full-population Spearman ρ is dominated by a sub-task that a trivial rule (for example, "has at least one transitive dependent in the projection") may solve almost completely. Please:

- **(i)** report such a rule's accuracy at separating inert from active components;
- **(ii)** evaluate the task in two parts, classifying active vs inert and then ranking within the active set;
- **(iii)** bring the active-stratum results from S25 into Table 7.

Overlap@K of 0.39–0.45 (Table 7) should be discussed against these baselines, not only against unweighted betweenness.

### M8. Learned results are not reproducible across devices at the scale of the claimed effects.

§8.2 reports that learned cells move across code revisions and devices by up to 0.172 in a fold mean (`HGT-QoS`: 0.041). Two different LOSO means for `HGT-QoS` coexist in the paper: 0.622 (CPU, Table 7) and 0.638 (GPU; S25, S30, and the registered contrast +0.085). The claimed effects are +0.073 (QoS channel), +0.035/+0.048 (hybrid vs learned) and −0.014 (typing), all of the same order as this drift. "Every comparison is made within one sweep" controls drift within a comparison, but an independent replicator cannot reproduce the reported values. Please:

- enforce deterministic kernels (`torch.use_deterministic_algorithms`, fixed thread counts), or report learned results as distributions over at least two devices;
- use one sweep's numbers consistently throughout the main text and supplement;
- state which conclusions survive the observed drift envelope.

### M9. The explanation layer (Contribution 4) is unvalidated, and the paper's own evidence partly contradicts it.

- **No validation of the attributions.** No developer study, no fault-injection check that the Fault Tolerance / Availability / Maintainability attributions point to effective remediations, and no quantitative result from the counterfactual verifier (§5.3), although the machinery exists.
- **The elicited weights make ranking worse.** Moving from a uniform prior toward the raw AHP judgments lowers ρ from 0.319 to 0.200 (S1). The composite Q(v) ranks at ρ = 0.205 under LOSO, below unweighted betweenness (0.349). On the pooled I_comp benchmark it is also below plain degree (S6).
- **The standards grounding is by name only.** The ISO/IEC 25010 link is a naming of graph metrics after sub-characteristics. It does not use ISO/IEC 25023 measures or any measurement-theoretic validation. The Tukey-fence CRITICAL flag (4.2% of components) is not evaluated against anything.

**Request.** Evaluate the attributions: apply the recommended remediation class through the counterfactual verifier and compare the impact reduction against a random or alternative remediation. Otherwise, demote the explanation layer from a contribution to a design proposal and remove "and explainable" from the abstract's final sentence.

### M10. Gaps in the related work.

The positioning omits several directly relevant lines of work:

- **Pre-deployment and test-time fault injection for microservices:** Filibuster (Meiklejohn et al., SoCC 2021); lineage-driven fault injection (Alvaro et al., SIGMOD 2015).
- **Architecture-level reliability prediction with failure propagation:** Brosch et al., *Architecture-Based Reliability Prediction with the Palladio Component Model* (TSE 2012).
- **Model-based fault-propagation analysis:** HiP-HOPS and failure-propagation-and-transformation calculi.
- **Static architecture recovery and misconfiguration detection for ROS:** ROSDiscover (Timperley et al., ICSA 2022).
- **Empirical microservice dependency structure:** Luo et al., Alibaba trace analysis (SoCC 2021).
- **Root-cause analysis surveys:** Soldani & Brogi (ACM CSUR 2022).
- **GNN surrogates for cascading failures in power grids.** PowerGraph is cited, but the closest methodological analogue, learned surrogates of cascade simulators, is not discussed.
- **Comparing learners over dependent folds:** Demšar (JMLR 2006); Benavoli et al. (JMLR 2017), whose Bayesian correlated t-test was designed exactly for this setting; Nadeau & Bengio's corrected resampled test, which is cited but not applied.

Please also check that MicroCause [37] is accurately characterised. To my knowledge it is a causal-discovery plus random-walk method (IWQoS 2020), not a GNN-based method at ICSME.

### M11. Verbosity and structure.

The paper is within the length limit but hard to read efficiently:

- **Repetition.** The same numbers recur five to seven times: 0.349 → 0.553 in the abstract, §1.4, the §7.1 summary, §7.1 body, §8.1, Table 11 and §9; likewise 0.657/0.683 and 0.760–0.805. Table 11 largely duplicates Table 7, and the first paragraph of the Conclusion restates the abstract.
- **Scattered registration narrative.** The registration and amendment story is spread across §1.3, §6.3, §7.1, §7.1.1 and §9. Consolidate it into one "Analysis plan and deviations" subsection.
- **Duplicated discussion.** §4.3 ("How much QoS is in this label") and §7.2 ("How much QoS the target can reward") make the same point.
- **Dependence on the supplement.** The main text depends on more than 40 pointers into S1–S30. Some claims can only be checked there, including the oracle behaviour, the matched-control arms and the hybrid-vs-learned tests. Bring the decision-relevant material into the body: the hybrid-vs-learned contrasts, the active-stratum column and the oracle parameters. Cut repetition to make room.

## 5. Recommendation

**Major Revision.**

**Justification.** The manuscript's reporting discipline — registration, amendment log, omnibus correction, active-stratum and test–retest analyses, artifact reconciliation, byte-identical corpus — is well above the Q1 norm. The typed, QoS-weighted pub-sub dependency representation is a useful contribution to the architecture-dependability literature. The paper is not acceptable as it stands, however, for five reasons:

1. **Construct.** The learned engines approximate a simulator that is cheaper than SaG's own feature extraction and that shares its dependency rules. The paper does not show value beyond that oracle (M1, M2).
2. **Central thesis.** The claim that *declared QoS contracts* drive the main gain is confounded by a generator that couples QoS to topology, and has not been separated from topic multiplicity (M3).
3. **Hybrids.** The headline hybrid result is adaptively designed, statistically indistinguishable from the underlying learned engines, and partly a variance-reduction artifact of being tested against its own prior (M4).
4. **Overstated framing.** RQ2 and RQ3 are framed more strongly than a non-significant registered effect and an n = 5, single-modeller transfer set allow (M5, M6).
5. **Reproducibility.** Learned figures drift across devices by as much as the effects being claimed (M8).

Each of these can be fixed with experiments the replication package is already set up to run:

- the reachability, CDI and GBM baselines;
- the multiplicity-only and QoS-permuted controls;
- retargeting on I_dyn;
- an independent re-model of two systems;
- deterministic reruns;

together with a recalibrated abstract and highlights. If the added baselines show that a weighted reachability count on the projection matches the engines, the paper's contribution narrows to the representation and closed-form analysis. It could still be publishable in JSS with that scope, but not with the current framing. I would be glad to review a revised version.

The revision was made in two passes: first against the comments below, then as a narrative pass on
the Abstract, Introduction, Discussion and Conclusion. Before any new number existed, the new
experiments were registered as **Amendment 7**. They run without GPU or database
(`reproduce/training_free_suite.py`). The harness first reproduced every published per-fold
Topo-QoS value to three decimals. All three of its decision rules applied, and the paper's thesis
changed accordingly.

The title is now *Software-as-a-Graph: Explicit Dependency Graphs Predict Cascading-Failure Impact in
Publish–Subscribe Systems Before Deployment*.
