# Referee Report (Round 2) — JSS Special Issue "AI Techniques for Performance, Reliability, and Sustainability of Modern Software Systems"

**Manuscript (revised):** *Software-as-a-Graph: Explicit Dependency Graphs Predict Cascading-Failure Impact in Publish–Subscribe Systems Before Deployment* (Yigit, Buzluca)
**Material reviewed:** revised `latex/` sources and PDF (25 pp.), supplement S1–S32 (27 pp.), `PREREGISTRATION.md` Amendment 7, `results/tf_*.json` and related artifacts, `reproduce/training_free_suite.py`, and the zero-shot scoring path (`reproduce/realworld_zeroshot.py`, `cli/loso_evaluate.py`)
**Date:** 2026-09-26

## 1. Summary

The revised paper models publish–subscribe architectures as typed multigraphs and derives a `DEPENDS_ON` projection. It then asks which rankers best order Applications by a simulated cascade-impact oracle. Registered controls added in revision (Amendment 7) change the paper's thesis. A plain count of a component's dependents on the projection now outranks every learned and hybrid engine: ρ = 0.764 vs 0.622–0.683 under leave-one-scenario-out, and 0.86–0.94 on five hand-authored system models. The earlier "QoS contracts drive the gain" claim is withdrawn in favour of a substrate explanation. The remaining contributions are:
- hybrid engines that beat a betweenness engine;
- a matched typing control showing an untyped GAT suffices;
- zero-shot transfer of learned engines;
- an ISO/IEC 25010 explanation layer, now presented as a design proposal.

## 2. Overall Impression & Assessment

**The revision is responsive and unusually honest.** Every first-round comment is addressed or explicitly scoped, and the author response maps each one. Several changes are real improvements:
- **Amendment 7:** new experiments registered before their run, a reproduction gate, decision rules that were applied even though they went against the original thesis, and a torch-free harness.
- **Clearer text:** the oracle is now described accurately, the independence guarantee is restated as procedural, the generator coupling is disclosed, and the analysis deviations are consolidated in §6.4.
- **References:** two unverifiable or misattributed references were corrected.

Transparency and reproducibility are now a clear strength.

**The revision also exposes a new central problem.** The headline result sits very close to a tautology of the evaluation design, and the paper does not yet show that it means anything beyond that design.
- The primary oracle propagates failure along the same Rule 1 and Rule 5 dependencies the projection encodes (§4.4).
- Its value is the mean feed loss over subscribers, and a publisher's direct dependents *are* its subscribers.
- A dependent count ranking this label well therefore validates the construction of the label as much as it validates SaG.
- The "94% inert-vs-active" result is the same observation: a component with no dependents cannot propagate failure in this oracle by construction.
- The only evidence independent of this construction (the behavioural oracle I_dyn, production incidents) is deferred to future work.

**Originality — reduced.** The winning ranker is architectural afferent coupling, i.e. fan-in. That is an established service metric: Absolute Importance of the Service (AIS; Rud, Schmietendorf and Dumke, 2006), and afferent coupling (Martin). The paper cites neither in this role. Contribution 1 (the typed model and projection) therefore carries most of the novelty. Yet only Rules 1 and 5 and the Application population are evaluated; Rules 2–4 and 6, brokers, hosts and libraries remain unvalidated.

**Significance — uncertain.** The practical recommendation "count dependents in CI" is sound and cheap. But it is only evidenced against a simulator built on the same dependencies. The AI components the special issue is about are now dominated, which makes the paper primarily a sobering result about AI on this benchmark. JSS explicitly welcomes negative results, but it raises a fit question for this *AI-techniques* special issue.

**Soundness — much improved, with residual problems:**
- a cross-harness comparison on the system models (M3);
- exploratory contrasts presented as headlines (M4);
- a large amount of now-secondary learned-engine material (M5);
- the QoS machinery keeps its prominence while having no demonstrated effect (M6).

**Presentation — good and within length.** 25 pages; abstract 248 words; highlights ≤ 80 characters; 7 keywords; declarations complete. A few leftovers from the old framing remain (minor comments).

## 3. Major Comments

### M1. The headline finding needs evidence that is independent of the oracle's construction.

The authors acknowledge (§4.4, §8.2) that the oracle re-derives Rules 1 and 5 and that counts are "the natural first reference". That makes Finding 1 an expected property of the benchmark rather than a discovery about dependability. The authors should score the same rankers against a target that does not share this construction. The replication package already has all of these:
- **the behavioural queue-flow oracle I_dyn** (SimPy; `reproduce/convergent_validity.py`, which already agrees with I* at ρ = 0.627);
- **the composite oracle I_comp**;
- **I\* with the library-propagation phase disabled.**

Three outcomes are possible, and each helps the paper:
- If InDeg and Reach still lead on I_dyn, Finding 1 becomes substantive.
- If the learned or QoS-aware engines lead there, the paper recovers its AI contribution and its QoS machinery (M6).
- If everything collapses, the paper must say so.

As it stands, "Explicit dependency graphs predict cascading-failure impact" (title) and "risk becomes measurable from architecture models alone" (abstract) overstate what has been shown. What has been shown is that dependency counts reproduce a dependency-based simulator.

### M2. Novelty relative to existing coupling and service-importance metrics.

InDeg on the projection is, at architectural level, the number of consumers of a component. That is AIS (Rud et al., 2006), afferent coupling Ca (Martin), and the "fan-in" in microservice coupling studies (see Bogner et al.'s 2017 review of service maintainability metrics). Reach is its transitive closure.

§2 should position the finding against this literature, and the contribution should be restated accordingly. The contribution then becomes:
- **(a)** the pub-sub-specific derivation that makes fan-in computable through topics, brokers and libraries (the projection);
- **(b)** empirical evidence that this fan-in dominates learned engines on this oracle.

It is not the discovery of a new ranker. Relatedly, the "decisive step" claim needs a comparison showing the projection matters. For example:
- InDeg computed on the raw multigraph (2-hop subscriber counts through topics);
- the same count without Rule 5.

Otherwise it is unclear what the typed model contributes beyond a subscriber count.

### M3. The system-model comparison mixes two scoring pipelines.

Table 7's system-model column and §7.3 compare two harnesses:
- dependency counts (0.863–0.938) come from the Amendment 7 harness;
- learned engines (0.760–0.805) come from the zero-shot pipeline.

The dagger note discloses that Topo-QoS scores 0.582 in the first and 0.526 in the second. The discrepancy is unexplained, and there is a plausible cause in the code. The LOSO path zeroes the articulation term, as §6.2 states. The zero-shot path, however, reads `ap_c_score` directly for Topo and loads structural metrics through `extract_structural_metrics_dict`, so the articulation term is likely non-zero there. §6.2's statement that the term "reads zero in the evaluated implementation" may then be false for Table 9.

The authors should:
- **(a)** state which articulation term each pipeline used;
- **(b)** reproduce Table 9's Topo and Topo-QoS columns in the new harness, or re-score the counts in the zero-shot pipeline;
- **(c)** only then compare Reach with GAT-QoS on these models.

Until then, "dependency counts transfer better than learned engines" is not a like-for-like result.

### M4. Registered versus exploratory: the headline contrasts are not the registered ones.

Amendment 7 registered the four new rankers **against Topo-QoS** (Holm within that family). The comparisons that carry the new narrative are exploratory and uncorrected:
- InDeg vs HGT-QoS, GAT-QoS and both hybrids (p = 0.0024–0.0049);
- everything on the system models.

Yet the abstract says "every headline contrast registered before its run", and places "above every learned engine" in the same sentence. Please:
- label these contrasts exploratory wherever they appear (abstract, §1.4, Table 11, §9);
- apply a correction across them (they would survive; four contrasts at p ≤ 0.005);
- remove "every headline contrast registered" or scope it.

Similarly, the inert-vs-active rule is a descriptive property of the oracle. It should not be presented as a detection capability ("identifies components whose failure reaches no one") without saying why it holds by construction.

### M5. The paper's weight no longer matches its findings.

Roughly half the body describes and evaluates engines that the paper's own Finding 1 dominates:
- the HGT architecture and the 16-D edge encoding (§4.1–4.2);
- the matched 2×2 (Table 8);
- the hybrids (§7.1, §7.1.1, Table 11).

The hybrids are "confirmed" only against Topo-QoS, a comparator that is itself beaten by the count on 12 of 12 folds. Presenting "hybrids significantly beat the closed-form engine" as Finding 3 selects a weak reference, which §6.3's own statistical guidance warns against.

There are two coherent options:
- **(a)** Reframe the paper as an empirical study, "what do learned rankers add over dependency counts on architecture graphs", and condense the learned-engine material.
- **(b)** Run the obvious next registered experiment: a hybrid anchored on the InDeg/Reach prior instead of Topo-QoS. Every reviewer will ask why the prior is the weaker engine.

As written, the narrative pass foregrounds the hybrids more than the evidence supports.

### M6. The QoS machinery has no demonstrated role.

Several components are still presented in detail, and they account for the "QoS-aware" framing that survives in §3 and the figures:
- Eqs. 2–4 (the topic weight);
- the AHP sub-weights and their consistency analysis;
- the 16-D QoS edge channel;
- the QoS severity ladder.

After Amendment 7:
- the declared contracts do not improve closed-form ranking (Finding 2);
- the edge channel's effect is not significant after correction and is confounded with relation identity (Finding 3);
- the oracle's ordering is 96.5% topological.

"Kept because the explanation layer and edge channel read them" (§3.2) is not a scientific justification. Either demonstrate a QoS effect on a QoS-sensitive target (I_dyn has QoS modes; see M1), or move the QoS derivation and AHP material to the supplement and say plainly that QoS plays no measured role in this study. The abstract's "which lowers adoption cost" should be scoped to this oracle.

### M7. Special-issue fit.

The special issue asks for AI techniques for performance, reliability and sustainability. In the revised paper, the recommended technique is a non-learned count, and the AI components are either dominated or evidenced only against weak references. That is a legitimate and valuable finding, but the authors should argue the fit explicitly, e.g. as a benchmark and negative result for AI4SE. Alternatively, the editor may prefer the regular JSS track. Resolving M1 in favour of learning on I_dyn would remove this concern.

## 4. Minor Comments

1. **Figures 1 and 3 predate the reframing.** They still show three engines (closed-form, learned, hybrid) and a "QoS-weighted `DEPENDS_ON` projection". Add the dependency counts to Figure 3, or note in the caption that Figure 3 depicts the learned pipeline only.
2. **§3.4 complexity claim.** It gives the CDI cost as O(|V|² + |V||E|), but the implementation uses a fixed 32-node BFS sample (`structural_analyzer.py`). State the sampled complexity. Note that the sample also explains the hash-seed nondeterminism the paper now reports.
3. **Inconsistent label-stability statistics.** §7.1 reports test–retest 0.811–1.000 (median 0.982) from the original label-stability artifact. The new reproduction gate computes a different statistic (minimum pairwise, e.g. 0.834 on Microservices). Name the statistic, and keep one.
4. **§8.1's example is invented.** "37 components depend on this service" is illustrative. Use a real component from the corpus (e.g. the top InDeg Application of ATM, with its I* rank) so the practical claim is concrete and checkable.
5. **Abstract wording.** "above the registered betweenness engine on all twelve": say "twelve folds". "In under a tenth of a second" should name the largest scenario.
6. **Keywords.** Add a term signalling the empirical or negative-result nature (e.g. "baselines" or "empirical study").
7. **§1.1 citation.** Perry and Wolf (1992) is cited as "architectural-smell research"; it is a foundations paper. Cite Garcia et al. alone, or rephrase.
8. **Table 7 layout.** Dagger values sit in the same column as non-dagger ones; after M3 the column should be single-source. Also, CDI appears in Table 7 but in no finding; consider moving it to the supplement.
9. **Table 11.** "Default CI gate for any architecture" generalizes beyond the evidence (pub-sub, synthetic plus five models). Scope it.
10. **§7.1 closing sentence.** "What learning adds … is not yet more than what a direct count of dependents captures." Good; mirror this sentence in the abstract instead of "Among learned engines, hybrids … are the only ones to beat it significantly", which reads as a success for a dominated method.
11. **Data Availability and reconciler count.** The statement now claims 537 reconciled figures, but in a fresh clone the reconciler checks 145, and the rest require the Zenodo bundle, which has not been re-cut with the Amendment 7 artifacts. Update the bundle before submission, and state how the 537 figure was obtained.
12. **Amendment 7 timing.** It was registered on the same day as the review that motivated it, in the authors' own repository. The paper says so, which is good; a third-party timestamp (OSF) for any further amendment would strengthen it.
13. **Supplement S7 and S6.** They still evaluate the explanation layer against I_comp with the release-gate discussion. That now reads as tangential; consider trimming.
14. **Title.** "Predict" implies forecasting real failures; "Rank simulated cascade impact" is what is shown (see M1).

## 5. Recommendation

**Major Revision.**

**Justification.** The authors did exactly what a rigorous revision should: they registered the requested controls, ran them, and let the results overturn their thesis. The paper is now more honest, more reproducible and better written than the first submission, and the practical message is useful. It is not yet acceptable for four reasons:

1. **Independence (M1).** The new headline is largely implied by an oracle built on the same dependencies, and no independent target has been tested, although one exists in the replication package.
2. **Novelty (M2).** The winning ranker is an established coupling metric, unpositioned in the literature.
3. **Comparability (M3).** The transfer comparison mixes pipelines, with an unexplained discrepancy.
4. **Emphasis (M4–M6).** Exploratory contrasts and dominated learned and QoS components are presented as headlines.

The two most valuable next steps are cheap:
- score all rankers against I_dyn;
- re-score the system models in one pipeline.

Depending on the I_dyn outcome, the paper becomes either a strong "simple baselines win" empirical study (well suited to JSS, possibly outside this special issue) or a paper in which learning and QoS demonstrably add value on a behavioural target.
