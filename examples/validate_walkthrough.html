Validate Walkthrough

A score that claims to predict failure impact has to be checked against something that measures it. Validate sets the quality model's Q(v) beside the impact the failure simulator measured for the same components, and asks a fixed sequence of questions. This page asks them in order for one 15-component system.

1 · align

Only components that both sides scored can be compared. The quality model scores everything; the failure sweep on the system layer fails applications, brokers and hosts. Anything else is dropped, with a warning, not counted as zero.

2 · rank agreement

The two scores are on different scales, so the question is whether they put components in the same order. Spearman's ρ measures that; Kendall's τ is reported beside it as a check.

critical in bothcritical in one onlycritical in neither
3 · how sure

A correlation over a handful of components can move a long way if one of them changes. Resampling the components with replacement many times shows how far.

4 · the critical few

A good overall order matters less than finding the components at the top. Each side marks its own top quartile as critical, and the validator counts the overlap.

5 · by type and by dimension

Pooling hides what happens inside each group and each dimension

Hosts, brokers and applications have different base rates, so a pooled correlation can be driven by the gap between types. Each quality dimension also has its own simulated counterpart.

6 · the gates

Three release gates decide the verdict. Three more are reported beside them and never change it. A gate whose input could not be measured says so.

7 · system health

One number per dimension, and one for the whole system

Health indices summarise the simulated impact across components. They are what the Prescribe step later tries to improve.
