SaG Method Walkthrough

The claim behind the method is that a component's position in the dependency graph predicts what happens when it fails, before anything is deployed. This page runs every stage of the pipeline on one 15-component air-traffic system and shows what each stage actually produced.

The map

Two pathways rank and explain; simulation checks both and gates every fix

Predict and Diagnose share no parameters. Predict says which components matter, Diagnose says why, and the triage bridge joins them by component id. Simulation never feeds either one at run time: it produces training labels offline and acts as the referee.

Stage 1 · Model

The architecture becomes a weighted graph, and dependencies are derived from it

The input is what an architect already has: who publishes and subscribes to which topic, which broker routes it, what runs where. Each topic gets a weight from its QoS contract, and components inherit weight from the topics they touch. Rules then derive who depends on whom: a subscriber depends on its publisher, an application on its broker and library, a host on the brokers its residents use.

publishes / subscribesroutesusesw = QoS-derived weight

Read the matrix by row: tracker depends on radar, both brokers and the library. Darker cells are heavier dependencies. Simulation never reads these derived edges; it works on the raw graph on the left.

Stage 2 · Analyze

Each component gets a vector of structural metrics, and nothing else

This stage is deterministic and closed-form. It measures position: how much of the system reaches a component, how many depend on it, whether it sits between others, how heavy its dependencies are. No scores, no judgement yet.

Stage 3 · Predict · pathway B

A ranking of likely blast radius, from a trained model or a closed-form reference

The trained model here is HGT-QoS, fitted on twelve other systems and applied to this one unseen. Beside it is Reach, the count of transitive dependents, which needs no training. The top three form the shortlist passed on to triage.

Stage 4 · Diagnose · pathway A

A deterministic quality profile explains the risk, and triage joins it to the ranking

The quality model turns the metric vector into reliability (fault tolerance and availability) and maintainability, combines them into Q(v), and assigns one of five levels. It also scans for architectural anti-patterns. No learning is involved.

The triage bridge: top three from pathway B, each joined to its pathway A profile by id

Stage 5 · Simulate

Each component is failed in turn on the raw graph to get ground truth

Two engines answer two different questions. I* is the cascade impact used as the training label for pathway B. I_comp is the system-wide loss used as the oracle for pathway A. The Mini-ATM Failure Lab shows both step by step.

Stage 6 · Validate

Predicted scores are compared with simulated impact and held to declared gates

Validation correlates Q(v) with I_comp for every component both sides scored, then checks a fixed set of gates. A gate that could not be measured is reported as not measured, never as a failure.

Stage 7 · Prescribe

Fixes are proposed, then accepted only if a counterfactual simulation confirms them

Each candidate edit is applied to a copy of the graph and re-simulated. It is accepted only when the impact reduction exceeds seed noise at every propagation threshold. An edit that looks sensible but cannot be distinguished from noise is rejected.

Reading the whole run

What this small system shows about the method

    How this was produced. saag.Client on a MemoryRepository loaded with examples/mini_atm.json: analyze, predict (HGT-QoS checkpoint, seed 42, app layer), diagnose with k=3, validate and prescribe on the system layer, plus FaultInjector with five seeds and the FailureSimulator system sweep. All options are defaults. Stage 8, Visualize, renders these same results as a dashboard and is not shown.

    Regenerate this page with python examples/run_method_walkthrough.py. Fifteen components is far below the 50 to 300 the method was developed on. Correlations over six to ten components have very wide intervals, so read this as a demonstration of how the stages connect, not as evidence of accuracy.
