Anti-Patterns and Prescribe Walkthrough

Finding a structural smell is the easy half. The harder half is knowing whether a proposed fix actually helps. This page scans a small air-traffic scenario for 19 known anti-patterns, turns the findings into candidate edits, and then lets counterfactual simulation decide which edits survive.

1 · detect

2 · propose

Three operators can change the architecture automatically. Only five catalogue patterns feed one of them; guidance for the other fourteen is advisory. Components the quality model rates critical or high can also trigger an operator directly.

3 · verify

Each candidate is applied alone to a copy of the system and every component is failed again, at three propagation thresholds and three seeds. The edit is accepted only if the average impact falls by more than the seed-to-seed noise at every threshold. Each bar below is one candidate at its worst threshold; the tick is the noise it had to beat.

4 · apply

The accepted edits are applied together and the whole system is simulated once more. The policy as a whole is kept only if the risk index falls.

Reading the result

What the closed loop did here

    How this was produced. python examples/run_prescribe_walkthrough.py on data/scenarios/atm_system_tiny.json: Client.predict(mode="rm") for the quality scores and the anti-pattern scan, then Client.prescribe with default settings (κ = 1, thresholds 0.1, 0.2 and 0.5, three seeds).

    The edits are structural proposals: a split topic or a new host has costs the simulator does not price. Treat an accepted edit as "worth an engineer's attention", not as a change to merge.
